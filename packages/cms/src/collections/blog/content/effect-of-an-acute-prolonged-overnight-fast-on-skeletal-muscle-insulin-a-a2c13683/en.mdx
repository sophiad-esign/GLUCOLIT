## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Does a single 16-hour overnight fast enhance postprandial insulin sensitivity or dietary protein-derived amino acid incorporation into skeletal muscle protein compared to a 10-hour fast in healthy men?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Healthy men (n=9; age 22.6 ± 3.5 years; BMI: 24.0 ± 2.5 kg/m²; HOMA-IR: 1.00 ± 0.54)
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Single 16-hour overnight fast vs. 10-hour fast
- **对照 / Comparison:** Postprandial insulin and anabolic sensitivity after a liquid meal containing intrinsically [1-13C]phenylalanine-labeled milk protein and dextrose
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Incorporation of dietary protein-derived [1-13C]phenylalanine into myofibrillar protein, forearm branched amino acid uptake, glucose uptake, and postprandial serum insulin response
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** No difference in protein incorporation or amino acid and glucose uptake between the two fasting durations; however, a lower postprandial serum insulin response was observed with the 16-hour fast.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was conducted on a small sample of healthy men, limiting the generalizability of the findings. The study did not investigate effects in susceptible populations such as older or insulin-resistant individuals.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes or insulin resistance, a single prolonged fast may lower the post-meal insulin response but does not appear to enhance muscle protein synthesis from food in healthy young adults. Further research is needed to understand the effects in different populations.

## 原文精华摘要

想象你经过漫长的一天，终于坐下来吃饭。 在比平常更长的禁食后，你的身体对食物有何反应？ 最近的一项研究揭示了这个问题的答案。 它比较了16小时禁食和10小时禁食对身体如何处理一餐的影响，重点关注胰岛素敏感性和肌肉蛋白质合成。

研究涉及健康男性，他们在每种禁食后都摄入了特别标记的蛋白质和糖饮料。 研究人员测量了这种饮料如何被纳入他们的肌肉，以及他们的身体在胰岛素和氨基酸摄取方面的反应。 有趣的是，他们发现，虽然更长时间的禁食确实降低了餐后胰岛素反应，但并没有增加肌肉从餐食中的蛋白质合成。

这表明，对于健康的年轻成年人来说，单次延长的禁食可能不会像我们想象的那样促进肌肉从食物中的构建。 然而，研究还暗示，年长或胰岛素抵抗的个体可能会有不同的反应，这指向了需要更多研究。

目前，如果你正在管理糖尿病前期或胰岛素抵抗，了解你的身体对不同禁食持续时间的反应可能是你健康策略中宝贵的一部分。

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you've been up for a long day and finally sit down for a meal. How does your body react to food after a longer-than-usual fast? A recent study sheds light on this question.

It compared the effects of a 16-hour fast to a 10-hour fast on how the body handles a meal, focusing on insulin sensitivity and muscle protein synthesis.

The study involved healthy men who, after each type of fast, consumed a specially labeled protein and sugar drink.

The researchers measured how this drink was incorporated into their muscles and how their bodies responded in terms of insulin and amino acid uptake.

Interestingly, they found that while a longer fast did lower the post-meal insulin response, it did not increase muscle protein synthesis from the meal's protein.

This suggests that for healthy young adults, a single extended fast may not boost muscle building from food as much as we might think.

However, the study also hints that older or insulin-resistant individuals might respond differently, pointing to the need for more research.

For now, if you're managing prediabetes or insulin resistance, understanding how your body reacts to different fasting durations could be a valuable part of your health strategy.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding how fasting affects insulin sensitivity and muscle protein synthesis is crucial for individuals with prediabetes or insulin resistance, as it can influence their dietary and lifestyle choices.

Practical Takeaways

- Prolonged fasting can lower post-meal insulin response but may not enhance muscle protein synthesis from food.
- A single extended fast does not seem to boost muscle building in healthy young adults.
- Different populations, such as older or insulin-resistant individuals, might experience different effects from fasting.
- It's important to consider how your body reacts to fasting when managing prediabetes or insulin resistance.
