## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the association between the appendicular skeletal muscle mass to visceral fat area ratio (SVR) and the risk of prediabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 4195 participants from the 2011-2018 National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES)
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Higher appendicular skeletal muscle mass to visceral fat area ratio (SVR)
- **对照 / Comparison:** Lower SVR
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Risk of prediabetes
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Higher SVR was significantly associated with reduced risk of prediabetes (adjusted OR 0.24; 95% CI: 0.19-0.31).
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is cross-sectional, limiting the ability to infer causation. Other factors not accounted for in the study may influence the relationship between SVR and prediabetes.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While a higher SVR is associated with a reduced risk of prediabetes, individuals should not consider this as medical advice. Maintaining muscle mass through strength training and a balanced diet may be beneficial, but further research is needed to establish causation.

## English Plain-Language Version

### Study Background Prediabetes is a condition where blood sugar levels are higher than normal but not high enough to be diagnosed as diabetes. Understanding how body composition influences this risk is crucial for early intervention.

### Core Findings Higher SVR is associated with reduced prediabetes risk, with gender-specific diagnostic thresholds.

### Your Interpretation and Critique The study highlights the importance of SVR in metabolic health. It suggests that maintaining muscle mass could reduce prediabetes risk, but causation cannot be inferred.

### Clinical/Business Insights Strength training and a balanced diet may help maintain muscle mass, potentially influencing prediabetes risk. Tools to track SVR could provide personalized feedback.

### Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the relationship between body composition and prediabetes risk is important for developing targeted interventions and lifestyle changes to prevent the progression to diabetes.

### Practical Takeaways

- A higher muscle to fat ratio is associated with a reduced risk of prediabetes.
- Maintaining muscle mass could be a strategy to reduce prediabetes risk.
- Observational studies like this cannot establish causation.
- Lifestyle changes, including strength training and diet, can influence body composition and potentially prediabetes risk.
