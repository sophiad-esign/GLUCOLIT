## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the critical period in the progression from prediabetes to type 2 diabetes, and how can diet influence this transition?
- **研究对象 / Population:** A cross-sectional cohort of 110 subjects divided into three groups: healthy controls (20), prediabetic (50), and type 2 diabetic (40), with 28 subjects completing a 2-year longitudinal follow-up.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** The study used a refined stratification strategy for HbA1c at intervals of 0.3%, integrating Dynamic Network Biomarker (DNB) theory with high-throughput proteomics technology, combined with longitudinal cohort validation and machine learning methods.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of HbA1c ranges and identification of 37 core DNB proteins through proteomics analysis.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** The HbA1c range of 6.0-6.2% was identified as a critical state for the transition from prediabetes to type 2 diabetes, with 37 core DNB proteins identified, mostly involved in extracellular matrix remodeling, inflammatory response, and insulin signaling pathways.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** The HbA1c range of 6.0-6.2% meets the criteria of the DNB theory, suggesting it as a critical state for the transition from prediabetes to type 2 diabetes. The predictive efficacy of the ADAM10 protein for clinical outcomes was significant, outperforming traditional indicators like HbA1c.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study's limitations include a small number of subjects, a short follow-up period, and a single center, single ethnic study design, limiting the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, there is a lack of functional validation of some important molecular targets.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** Readers with prediabetes should be aware of the critical HbA1c range of 6.0-6.2% as a potential turning point and consider dietary changes that could influence the identified protein expressions and pathways. However, they should also be cautious about overclaiming the results due to the study's limitations and consider these findings as part of a broader strategy for managing prediabetes.
