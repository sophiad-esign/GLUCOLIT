## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the relationship between environmental exposures and the incidence, remission, and progression of prediabetes phenotypes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Data were derived from the KORA cohort in Southern Germany (2006-2022), including participants with prediabetes.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Annual values of particulate matter (PM), light at night (LAN), normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI), imperviousness (IMP), and air temperature variation (Tsd) were included.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Complementary log-log regression models were fitted for associations between IQR changes in exposures and the incidence of prediabetes phenotypes, remission to normal glucose tolerance, or progression to type 2 diabetes.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Consistent associations of air pollutants and the built environment with iIGT or IFG+IGT were observed. Co-exposure to environmental mixtures was associated with increased risks of iIFG, iIGT, and IFG+IGT.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** An IQR increase in PM2.5 showed an HR of 1.89 (95% CI 1.07, 3.31) for the risk of IFG+IGT and 1.59 (95% CI 1.03, 2.46) with a decrease in NDVI. Lower NDVI, higher LAN, and higher IMP were associated with reduced remission of iIFG.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study shows associations but not causation. There is potential heterogeneity in environmental exposures and prediabetes phenotypes, and more research is needed to understand these relationships fully.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While this study indicates a link between environmental factors and prediabetes, it does not provide a direct cause-and-effect relationship. It suggests that environmental factors might be part of the reason some people develop prediabetes, but further research is needed to confirm these findings and understand the mechanisms involved.

Prediabetes is a warning sign that you might develop type 2 diabetes. It includes conditions like impaired fasting glucose and impaired glucose tolerance.

The study looked at how factors like air pollution, temperature, and green spaces could affect whether people with prediabetes get better or worse.

The researchers found that higher levels of air pollution were linked to a higher risk of developing certain types of prediabetes. They also found that having less green space in your neighborhood could be a problem.

This suggests that where you live might affect your health in more ways than you think.

It's important to understand that this study shows a link, not a cause. It means that these environmental factors might be part of the reason some people develop prediabetes, but it doesn't prove that they are the cause.

There's still a lot we don't know, and more research is needed.

So, what can you do? If you have prediabetes, it's a good idea to talk to your doctor about your environment and how it might be affecting your health.

In the end, it's about understanding that our health is influenced by many factors, including where we live. It's one more piece of the puzzle in managing prediabetes and preventing type 2 diabetes.
