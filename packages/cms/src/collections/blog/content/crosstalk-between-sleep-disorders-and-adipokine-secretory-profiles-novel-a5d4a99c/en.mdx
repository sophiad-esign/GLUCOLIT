## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How do sleep disorders affect the risk of metabolic diseases, particularly in individuals with prediabetes and insulin resistance?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Individuals with sleep disorders, including sleep deprivation, obstructive sleep apnea, and circadian rhythm disruption.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Sleep disturbances and their impact on adipokine secretion, including leptin, adiponectin, interleukin-6 (IL-6), and angiopoietin-like protein 4 (ANGPTL4).
- **对照 / Comparison:** The bidirectional relationship between sleep disorders and metabolic diseases, focusing on the mediating role of dysregulated adipokine secretion.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Insulin resistance, dyslipidemia, and chronic low-grade inflammation, which elevate metabolic disease risk.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Sleep deprivation and fragmentation promote leptin dysregulation and reduce adiponectin levels, while OSA-driven intermittent hypoxia upregulates IL-6 and ANGPTL4. These alterations contribute to insulin resistance and metabolic disease risk.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is a review and does not provide direct causal evidence but synthesizes current evidence on the topic. The complexity of sleep and metabolic interactions may limit the generalizability of findings.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes and insulin resistance, addressing sleep disorders could be a significant step in managing their metabolic health. However, it's important to recognize that while there is a strong association, direct causation has not been established, and individual responses to sleep interventions may vary.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine trying to run a marathon without proper rest. Your body would struggle to perform at its best. Similarly, sleep is not just rest; it's a critical time for your body to regulate hormones that control metabolism.

A recent study highlights how sleep disorders can disrupt these processes, increasing the risk of metabolic diseases like prediabetes and type 2 diabetes.

Sleep deprivation and conditions like sleep apnea can alter hormone levels, leading to insulin resistance and inflammation, which are key risk factors for metabolic disorders.

It's a two-way street: obesity and diabetes can also worsen sleep, creating a harmful cycle. The good news is that improving sleep quality can break this cycle, offering a practical approach to managing metabolic health.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Sleep is often overlooked in discussions about metabolic health, but it plays a crucial role in hormone regulation and overall metabolic balance.

Understanding the connection between sleep disorders and metabolic diseases can help individuals with prediabetes and insulin resistance make informed decisions about their health.

Practical Takeaways

- Sleep disorders can significantly impact metabolic health, increasing the risk of prediabetes and type 2 diabetes.
- Improving sleep quality may be a practical approach to managing metabolic risk.
- Sleep and metabolic diseases engage in a vicious cycle, which can be mitigated by addressing sleep issues.
- Public health strategies that promote 'quality sleep' alongside nutrition and physical activity could reduce the burden of metabolic and sleep disorders.
