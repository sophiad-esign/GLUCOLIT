## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How does glucose metabolism differ between overweight and lean men, and what are the implications for prediabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Overweight men (n=9) and lean men (n=7) were studied.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Arteriovenous difference sampling across the forearm was used to measure metabolic substrate fluxes over three meals and after an overnight fast during a 24-hour cycle.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Net metabolic substrate fluxes were compared between overweight and lean men.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Overweight men had higher fasting and postprandial insulin levels and 3.6-fold higher forearm glucose uptake compared to lean men.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Overweight men exhibited elevated basal glucose uptake and increased alanine release, suggesting a pathway towards enhanced hepatic gluconeogenesis.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was conducted over a 24-hour period and only included 16 participants, limiting the generalizability of the findings.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** These findings suggest that overweight individuals with prediabetes may have increased glucose metabolism, which could impact their risk of developing type 2 diabetes and should be considered when planning exercise and dietary interventions.

## English Plain-Language Version

Feeling sluggish after a heavy meal? It might be more than just indigestion. Prediabetes can make your body less flexible in how it uses energy, relying heavily on glucose.

A recent study compared overweight men, who often have higher insulin levels, to lean men.

It found that overweight men had much higher glucose uptake, even when fasting, challenging the idea that insulin resistance means less glucose uptake.

This could be a sign that their bodies are adapting to use more glucose, possibly to make up for less fatty acid use. Overweight men also showed increased alanine release, which might lead to more glucose production in the liver.

Understanding these changes can help us manage prediabetes better through exercise and diet.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Prediabetes is a critical health issue affecting many people worldwide. Understanding how exercise and diet can influence glucose metabolism is essential for managing this condition and preventing the progression to type 2 diabetes.

Practical Takeaways

- Exercise can help manage prediabetes by improving how your body uses glucose.
- Overweight individuals may have higher glucose uptake, which could be a sign of early metabolic changes.
- Increased alanine release might indicate a shift towards more glucose production in the liver.
- Understanding your body's metabolism can guide personalized diet and exercise plans.
