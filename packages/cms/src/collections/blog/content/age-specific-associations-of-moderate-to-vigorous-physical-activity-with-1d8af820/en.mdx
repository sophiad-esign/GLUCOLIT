## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How does moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) affect the risk of developing type 2 diabetes, and does this relationship vary by age and prediabetes status?
- **研究对象 / Population:** UK Biobank participants without diabetes at baseline (N = 87,975; average age 62.7 years; 57% women)
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) volume and timing
- **对照 / Comparison:** Lower versus higher MVPA volume, later-day versus earlier-day MVPA timing
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Incident type 2 diabetes
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Stronger associations between MVPA volume and lower type 2 diabetes risk were observed among participants younger than the median population age (<62.7 years) and those without prediabetes. The relative association between higher MVPA volume and lower type 2 diabetes risk attenuated with age and was weaker among individuals with prediabetes, whereas absolute risk reductions were larger among older and metabolically higher-risk individuals. No clear effect modification was observed for the association between MVPA timing and type 2 diabetes risk.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is observational and does not establish causation. The findings are specific to the UK Biobank population and may not be generalizable to all populations.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While the study shows that MVPA can significantly reduce the risk of type 2 diabetes, especially for younger individuals and those without prediabetes, it also suggests that the benefits of exercise may vary by age and metabolic health. This information can help individuals with prediabetes make informed decisions about their exercise routines to manage their risk.

## English Plain-Language Version

Facing prediabetes? Exercise might be a key part of your health strategy.

According to a study in the UK Biobank, exercise volume and timing can influence the risk of type 2 diabetes, especially for younger individuals and those without prediabetes. The study included nearly 88,000 participants, average age 62.

7, without diabetes at the start. It found that the benefits of exercise were most noticeable in younger participants and those without prediabetes.

As age increased, the relative risk reduction from exercise decreased, but the absolute risk reduction increased, particularly for those at higher metabolic risk.

This suggests that while the immediate benefits of exercise might be less pronounced as you age, the absolute protection against diabetes can still be significant, especially for those with other risk factors.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Exercise is a critical component of managing prediabetes and preventing the progression to type 2 diabetes.

Understanding how different types and amounts of exercise can impact this risk is essential for individuals looking to improve their health outcomes.

Practical Takeaways

- Exercise can significantly reduce the risk of type 2 diabetes, particularly for those under 62.7 years old and without prediabetes.
- The protective effect of exercise against type 2 diabetes may decrease with age, but the absolute risk reduction increases, especially for those at higher metabolic risk.
- There is no clear evidence that the timing of exercise affects the risk of type 2 diabetes, suggesting that any time is a good time to exercise.
- Exercise should be tailored to individual health needs and circumstances, considering both relative and absolute risk measures.
- Making exercise a regular part of your life can significantly improve your health outcomes, even as you age.
