## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Does aerobic exercise mitigate fasting-induced glucose intolerance?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 16 healthy adults (8 males and 8 females, aged 24±4 years)
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** 48-hour fasting with and without daily cycling (50-min moderate-intensity continuous training + 5×1-min high-intensity interval training)
- **对照 / Comparison:** Glycaemic responses before, during, and after fasting, and 24 hours after refeeding
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Capillary glucose and continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) incremental area under the curve (iAUC) during the 2-hour mixed meal tolerance test
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Exercise did not prevent the increase in blood sugar levels after fasting and refeeding. Glucose intolerance persisted 24 hours after refeeding.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was conducted over a short period (48 hours) and may not reflect long-term fasting effects. The sample size was small and limited to healthy adults.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While exercise is beneficial for overall health, it may not be effective in managing glucose intolerance during short-term fasting. It's important to consider individual responses and consult with a healthcare provider for personalized advice.

## 原文精华摘要

想象一下，你已经禁食了几天，现在准备开斋。 你的身体对食物的反应会是怎样的？ 最近的一项研究揭示了这个问题的答案。 结果表明，无论你在禁食期间是否进行运动，你在开斋后对身体的反应都是相似的。 这项研究涉及16名健康成年人，他们禁食48小时，有和没有每天运动，然后监测他们的血糖水平。

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you've been fasting for a couple of days and you're about to break your fast. How does your body react to food after such a long wait? A recent study sheds light on this question.

It turns out that whether you exercise or not during your fast, your body's response to food after breaking the fast is similar.

The study involved 16 healthy adults who fasted for 48 hours, with and without daily exercise, and then had their blood sugar levels monitored.

The key finding was that exercise did not prevent the increase in blood sugar levels after fasting and refeeding.

This suggests that while exercise is beneficial for overall health, it may not be the solution for managing glucose intolerance during fasting.

It's important to note that the study's results apply to short-term fasting and may not be the same for longer periods.

Also, the study showed that women had a greater increase in blood sugar levels after refeeding compared to men, indicating that gender might play a role in glucose metabolism.

Remember, this information is not a prescription for managing your health but a guide to understanding recent research findings. Always consult with a healthcare professional for personalized advice.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding how the body responds to fasting and exercise is crucial for individuals with prediabetes or insulin resistance, as it can influence their dietary and lifestyle choices.

Practical Takeaways

- Exercise does not appear to prevent glucose intolerance after short-term fasting and refeeding.
- Blood sugar levels can remain elevated even 24 hours after refeeding.
- Gender differences may exist in how the body responds to fasting and refeeding.
- It's crucial to discuss personal health strategies with a healthcare provider.
