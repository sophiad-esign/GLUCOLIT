## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How do circadian behavior and metabolic health indicators affect fertility in women with overweight or obesity?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 283 females with a BMI of 25-40 kg/m² participating in the HELMS program at KK Women's and Children's Hospital, Singapore.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Assessment of circadian behavior using questionnaires, tracking diaries, and digital wearables; evaluation of metabolic health through anthropometry, body fat composition, metabolic syndrome score, and insulin resistance index.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of changes in circadian behavior and metabolic health indicators over a 3-month period with subsequent reproductive outcomes within a year.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Clinical pregnancy rate and fecundability within one year of conception attempts.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** The study aims to identify associations between changes in circadian behavior and metabolic health indicators and subsequent reproductive outcomes.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is ongoing, and findings are not yet available. The sample size is limited to a specific population in Singapore.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** This study suggests that managing circadian behaviors and metabolic profiles may benefit women with overweight or obesity who are experiencing unexplained infertility. However, the results are not yet known, and the applicability to other populations is uncertain.

## 原文精华摘要

生育力与人体的生物钟和代谢过程密切相关。 这些系统的紊乱可能会影响生育力，特别是在BMI较高的女性中。 新加坡的一项研究正在深入探讨这些联系。

研究人员将在三个月内跟踪女性的饮食习惯和睡眠模式，以观察这些习惯的变化是否能在一年内提高生育力。

该研究将使用问卷和可穿戴设备来监测日常习惯，并通过身体测量和血液测试来评估代谢健康。

了解这些联系可以为试图怀孕的女性提供个性化建议，可能通过生活方式调整提高生育力。

在等待研究结果的同时，女性可以考虑监测自己的饮食和睡眠习惯，并咨询医疗保健提供者进行重大的生活方式改变。

## English Plain-Language Version

Human reproduction is intricately linked to the body's internal clock and metabolic processes. Disruptions in these systems can affect fertility, particularly in women with higher BMIs.

A study in Singapore is delving into these connections.

Researchers will track eating and sleeping patterns of women over three months to see if changes in these habits can boost fertility within a year.

The study will use questionnaires and wearables to monitor daily routines and assess metabolic health through body measurements and blood tests.

Understanding these links could lead to personalized advice for women trying to conceive, potentially improving fertility through lifestyle adjustments.

While awaiting the study's results, women can consider monitoring their own eating and sleeping habits and consult healthcare providers for significant lifestyle changes.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding how lifestyle factors like diet and sleep affect fertility is essential, especially for women with prediabetes, insulin resistance, or early metabolic risk.

Practical Takeaways

- Circadian behavior and metabolic health are crucial for fertility.
- Lifestyle adjustments may improve fertility in women with higher BMIs.
- Personalized preconception care could be enhanced by understanding daily routines.
