## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What are the changes in metabolic parameters among Filipino seafarers before and after a long voyage, and what demographic and occupational factors predict these changes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 402 male Filipino seafarers
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Prolonged deployments on ships with irregular schedules, limited physical activity, and constrained dietary diversity
- **对照 / Comparison:** Pre- and post-embarkation medical records
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Significant increases in BMI, fasting blood sugar, impaired fasting glucose, and hyperuricemia; decrease in serum glutamic-pyruvic transaminase (SGPT) levels
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Post-embarkation increases in BMI, fasting blood sugar, impaired fasting glucose, and hyperuricemia; decrease in SGPT levels; predictors of these changes include post-embarkation timing, engine department assignment, and older age
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study shows associations but does not prove causation; changes could be due to multiple factors beyond diet and activity levels
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** Seafarers should be aware of the potential metabolic health risks associated with their work environment and consider proactive health measures, including a balanced diet, regular physical activity, and regular health monitoring.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine being at sea for months, with limited food choices and irregular schedules. It's not just about missing home; it can affect your health.

A study of Filipino seafarers found that after a long voyage, their body mass index, fasting blood sugar, and levels of uric acid increased, while liver enzyme levels decreased.

This highlights the need for a proactive approach to health, especially for those working in the engine department or older seafarers.

The study didn't provide specific dietary advice, but it's clear that a balanced diet and regular physical activity are crucial.

For seafarers, this might mean planning meals with more whole grains, lean proteins, and vegetables, and finding ways to stay active despite the constraints of ship life.

It's also important to monitor health regularly and discuss any changes with a healthcare provider.

While the study shows associations, it doesn't prove causation. The changes in metabolic health could be due to many factors, not just diet and activity levels.

However, it's a reminder that lifestyle choices can have a significant impact on health, especially in a challenging environment like working at sea.

Practical steps for seafarers could include setting a regular sleep schedule, finding time for exercise every day, and planning meals that are rich in nutrients but easy to prepare and store on a ship.

It's also crucial to stay in touch with healthcare providers and discuss any health concerns that arise during their time at sea.

In summary, for seafarers, maintaining metabolic health is about more than just surviving at sea; it's about thriving despite the challenges.

By making smart choices about diet, exercise, and sleep, they can take control of their health and reduce the risks associated with their unique work environment.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Metabolic health is a critical concern for seafarers due to the unique challenges of their work environment, including limited dietary options and irregular schedules.

Understanding the impact of these factors on health can help seafarers make informed decisions about their diet and lifestyle to mitigate risks.

Practical Takeaways

- Seafaring work can significantly impact metabolic health, with potential increases in BMI, fasting blood sugar, and uric acid levels.
- A balanced diet and regular physical activity are crucial for seafarers to maintain their health while at sea.
- Regular health monitoring and discussions with healthcare providers can help seafarers manage their health effectively.
- Practical steps include setting a regular sleep schedule, daily exercise, and planning nutritious and easy-to-prepare meals.
- Seafarers should take control of their health by making smart lifestyle choices to reduce the risks associated with their work environment.
