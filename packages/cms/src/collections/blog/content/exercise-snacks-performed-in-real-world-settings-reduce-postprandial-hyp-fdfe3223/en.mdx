## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Can short bursts of intense exercise, known as exercise snacks, improve glucose regulation in individuals with type 2 diabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 31 previously inactive individuals with well-controlled type 2 diabetes, not on insulin therapy, with an average age of 58 years, BMI of approximately 31 kg/m2, and HbA1c of approximately 48 mmol/mol (6.6%).
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Four 1-minute bouts of vigorous bodyweight exercise over two consecutive days.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Two consecutive days of no exercise.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Primary outcome was mean glucose during each 48-hour condition. Secondary outcomes included 2-hour postprandial glucose responses and markers of glycaemic variability.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** The exercise snacks condition led to small but statistically significant improvements in standard deviation, coefficient of variation, mean amplitude of glycaemic excursions, and time in tight range compared to the control condition. Postprandial glucose responses were also significantly lower after meals during the exercise condition.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study did not find a statistically significant difference in mean glucose levels between the two conditions. The sample size was relatively small, and the study was conducted over a short period.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with type 2 diabetes who are not very active, incorporating brief, intense exercise sessions into their daily routine may help improve glycaemic control and reduce postprandial hyperglycaemia. However, it's important to note that the overall impact on mean glucose levels was not significant, and the long-term effects of this intervention are not known.

## 原文精华摘要

2型糖尿病患者经常寻找控制血糖水平的方法。 发表在《糖尿病学》上的研究提供了有希望的见解。 它表明，短暂的剧烈运动，被称为“运动小食”，可以提供帮助。 这些是可以轻松融入日常生活的快速、一分钟的活动。 研究表明，这些运动小食可以改善身体如何处理餐后糖以及整体血糖变异性。

研究结果特别相关，因为它们解决了糖尿病患者的餐后高血糖问题，这是一个常见问题，如果管理得不好，可能会导致并发症。 研究表明，每天只需四分钟的剧烈运动，分散在短暂的会话中，就可以显著降低餐后血糖水平和整体更好地控制血糖。

对于那些不太活跃的2型糖尿病患者来说，考虑在日常中添加短暂的剧烈运动可能是有益的。 在开始任何新的锻炼计划之前，总是建议与医疗服务提供者咨询。 这项研究突出了将简短的高强度运动融入日常生活以更好地管理糖尿病的一个实用方法。

## English Plain-Language Version

Individuals with type 2 diabetes often seek ways to manage their blood sugar levels. A study published in Diabetologia offers promising insights. It suggests that short, intense exercise sessions, known as 'exercise snacks,' can help.

These are quick, one-minute bursts of activity that can be easily incorporated into daily routines.

The research indicates that these exercise snacks can lead to improvements in how the body handles sugar after meals and overall blood sugar variability.

The study's findings are particularly relevant as they address postprandial hyperglycemia, a common issue for people with diabetes that can lead to complications if not well managed.

The study demonstrated that just four minutes of intense exercise a day, spread across short sessions, can result in significant drops in blood sugar levels after meals and better overall blood sugar control.

For those with type 2 diabetes who are not very active, considering adding short bursts of intense exercise to their routine could be beneficial.

It's always recommended to consult with a healthcare provider before starting any new exercise regimen.

This study highlights a practical approach to integrating brief, high-intensity exercises into daily life for better diabetes management.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Managing blood sugar levels is crucial for individuals with type 2 diabetes to prevent complications.

The study provides a practical approach that can be easily integrated into daily routines, offering a potential solution for better glucose control.

Practical Takeaways

- Brief, intense exercise sessions, or 'exercise snacks,' may help manage blood sugar levels.
- Postprandial hyperglycemia is a common issue for people with diabetes and can lead to complications.
- Intense exercise for just four minutes a day can lead to significant improvements in blood sugar control.
- It's important to consult with a healthcare provider before starting any new exercise regimen.
- Integrating brief, high-intensity exercises into daily life could be a practical approach to better diabetes management.
