## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What are the effects of a 12-month combined aerobic and resistance training program on physical fitness, cardiometabolic health, and quality of life in middle-aged adults with overweight/obesity at high risk of metabolic syndrome?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 192 overweight or obese, professionally active adults (mean age 46.2 ± 7.2 years; BMI 30.04 ± 2.78 kg/m2; 137 men, 55 women) in Poland.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** A 12-month supervised aerobic-resistance program.
- **对照 / Comparison:** No formal control group, but an age- and BMI-matched comparison group was retrospectively drawn from the same medical database for descriptive reference.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Cooper test performance, anthropometry and body composition, metabolic markers including insulin resistance HOMA-IR, HeartScore2 10-year cardiovascular mortality risk, health-related quality of life HRQoL, and quality-adjusted life years QALYs.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Cooper distance increased by ∼33%, BMI and body weight decreased modestly, central adiposity improved more markedly, HOMA-IR fell by ∼18%, triglycerides and uric acid decreased, HDL-cholesterol increased, blood pressure declined by ∼3%, HeartScore2 decreased by 13.3%, HRQoL utility improved from 0.66 to 0.76, corresponding to an estimated 1.04 QALY gain.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study lacked a formal control group, and the results may not be generalizable to populations outside of Poland or those with different baseline characteristics.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes and obesity, a structured 12-month exercise program can lead to significant improvements in physical fitness, cardiometabolic health, and quality of life, supporting its role in primary prevention and health-risk reduction.

## English Plain-Language Version

Embarking on a structured exercise program can lead to substantial health benefits.

In Poland, a study demonstrated that middle-aged adults with overweight or obesity experienced improvements in cardiometabolic health and quality of life after a year of combined aerobic and resistance training.

The 12-month intervention involved 192 participants and resulted in a 33% increase in Cooper test performance, a decrease in BMI and body weight, and enhancements in insulin resistance and other metabolic markers.

Incorporating structured physical activity into daily routines can lead to better physical fitness and overall well-being.

The study's outcomes highlight the importance of long-term exercise commitment for reducing cardiovascular risk and improving quality-adjusted life years.

Finding the right exercise routine and maintaining it is key. The study's results translate to real-life benefits such as increased energy, better sleep, and an enhanced sense of well-being.

It's also essential to consult healthcare providers before starting any new exercise program to ensure it's safe and suitable for individual needs.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

This study provides real-world evidence on the benefits of structured exercise for individuals with prediabetes and obesity, two common risk factors for metabolic syndrome.

The findings can guide individuals in making informed decisions about their health and encourage the adoption of physical activity as a preventive measure.

Practical Takeaways

- Structured exercise can significantly improve cardiometabolic health and quality of life.
- Long-term commitment to physical activity leads to substantial health benefits.
- Combining regular exercise with a balanced diet and good sleep habits is essential.
- Consulting healthcare providers before starting a new exercise program is crucial.
- Action taken today can lead to a healthier tomorrow.
