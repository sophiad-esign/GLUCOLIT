## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What are the molecular subtypes of prediabetes and how are they associated with the risk of developing diabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 538 participants with impaired fasting glucose (IFG) from the Nutrition and Health of Aging Population in China (NHAPC) cohort.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Proteomic analysis of plasma proteins to identify subtypes of IFG.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of molecular features and diabetes risk between two identified IFG subtypes.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Identification of two IFG subtypes with distinct molecular features and associations with future diabetes risk.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** A high-risk IFG subtype characterized by enrichment of glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, insulin signaling, and neutrophil degranulation, along with a dyslipidemic lipidomic profile, showed a greater decline in insulin sensitivity and higher incident diabetes.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study's findings are based on a specific cohort and may not be generalizable to all populations. The molecular subtypes were identified through proteomic analysis, which may have limitations in terms of sensitivity and specificity.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While this research provides insights into the molecular heterogeneity of prediabetes, it suggests that targeted lifestyle interventions, such as dietary changes and physical activity, could be beneficial for individuals with the high-risk IFG subtype to reduce their risk of developing diabetes.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're at a crossroads, unsure whether the path you're on will lead to diabetes. The good news is, understanding your prediabetes subtype can guide you towards healthier choices.

Recent research identified two molecular subtypes of impaired fasting glucose (IFG), one with a higher risk of progressing to diabetes.

This high-risk subtype is characterized by changes in plasma proteins associated with glycolysis, insulin signaling, and lipid metabolism.

By focusing on diet and lifestyle, you can influence these biological processes and potentially reduce your risk of developing diabetes.

For instance, adopting a meal structure that includes balanced portions of complex carbohydrates, lean proteins, and healthy fats can help regulate glucose and lipid levels.

Incorporating regular physical activity, ensuring adequate sleep, and monitoring your body's responses can further support your health journey.

It's important to remember that while these lifestyle changes can be beneficial, they should be discussed with a healthcare provider to ensure they are appropriate for your specific condition.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Prediabetes is a critical stage where lifestyle interventions can significantly impact the progression to diabetes. Understanding the molecular subtypes of IFG can help tailor these interventions more effectively.

Practical Takeaways

- Understanding your prediabetes subtype can guide personalized lifestyle changes.
- A balanced diet with complex carbohydrates, lean proteins, and healthy fats can help manage glucose and lipid levels.
- Regular physical activity and adequate sleep are crucial components of diabetes prevention.
- It's important to discuss lifestyle changes with a healthcare provider to ensure they are appropriate for your condition.
