## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Do meal replacements combined with lifestyle interventions provide additional benefits for weight and visceral fat reduction in prediabetic individuals?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 100 prediabetic participants with BMI between 25-35 kg/m2, aged 18-70 years.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Lifestyle intervention combined with meal replacements (LSI + MR group) versus lifestyle intervention alone (LSI group).
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of weight, visceral fat area, blood pressure, and biochemical indicators between the two groups after 12 weeks.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Greater percentage reductions in weight and improvements in certain metabolic indicators in the LSI + MR group.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** The LSI + MR group showed greater percentage reductions in weight compared to the LSI group. Benefits varied by baseline visceral fat area.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was conducted over a 12-week period, and the sample size was limited to 100 participants. The study's results may not be generalizable to all prediabetic individuals.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes and obesity, incorporating meal replacements into a lifestyle intervention program may lead to greater reductions in weight and visceral fat compared to lifestyle changes alone. However, the benefits may vary based on individual starting conditions, and consultation with a healthcare provider is essential for personalized management.

## English Plain-Language Version

Managing prediabetes and obesity can be challenging. A study presents meal replacements as a tool that, when combined with lifestyle changes, can help reduce weight and harmful belly fat more effectively.

This approach not only aids in weight loss but also improves overall health and reduces the risk of developing diabetes.

Meal replacements provide controlled calories and nutrients, which are beneficial for managing weight and metabolic health.

The study indicated that individuals who used meal replacements along with lifestyle changes experienced greater reductions in weight and visceral fat area compared to those who only made lifestyle changes.

The benefits of meal replacements were found to vary based on the amount of visceral fat at the start of the study.

For those with less belly fat, meal replacements improved post-meal blood sugar levels, while for those with more belly fat, they led to greater reductions in weight, triglycerides, and cholesterol.

Personalized strategies are crucial for managing prediabetes effectively. What works for one person may not work for another. The key is finding the right combination of strategies that work best for each individual.

Meal replacements should be part of a comprehensive lifestyle intervention under the guidance of a healthcare provider. It's about more than just diet; it's about how you live your life.

The study's findings highlight the need for personalized approaches in managing prediabetes. It's not just about the numbers on the scale, but how you feel and the quality of your life.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Prediabetes is a significant health concern that, if left unmanaged, can progress to type 2 diabetes.

This study is relevant because it provides evidence that meal replacements, when combined with lifestyle interventions, can offer additional benefits in managing weight and reducing visceral fat, which are key risk factors for diabetes and
