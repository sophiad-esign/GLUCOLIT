## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the accessibility of the National Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) for residents with prediabetes in New York State?

- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Access to the National Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP)
- **对照 / Comparison:** Variation in access by race/ethnicity and county-level prediabetes prevalence

## English Plain-Language Version

### Study Background Prediabetes is a health condition that affects many adults in New York State, with a higher prevalence among certain racial/ethnic groups and those with lower socioeconomic status.

The National Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) is designed to help prevent or delay the onset of type 2 diabetes.

### Core Findings The study reveals that 84. 3% of residents with prediabetes in New York State live within a 30-minute drive of a DPP site, indicating widespread access but also highlighting areas of unavailability.

### Your Interpretation and Critique The study underscores the importance of geographical access to DPP, yet it does not address the quality or effectiveness of these programs.

It also points to disparities in access based on race and ethnicity, suggesting a need for targeted expansion efforts.

### Clinical/Business Insights The study's findings suggest that while most residents with prediabetes have access to DPP, there are areas, particularly in high prediabetes prevalence counties, that require increased program availability.

This presents an opportunity for health tech companies to partner with communities to enhance accessibility.

### Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the accessibility of diabetes prevention programs is crucial for public health efforts aimed at reducing the incidence of type 2 diabetes, especially among high-risk populations.

### Practical Takeaways

- Most residents with prediabetes in New York State have access to the DPP.
- There are still areas with limited access to DPP, particularly in counties with high prediabetes rates.
- Access to DPP varies by race and ethnicity, with greater access among Black, Asian, and Hispanic residents compared to White residents.
- Further expansion of DPP sites is needed to increase accessibility.
