## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What are the effects of 16/8 Time-Restricted Eating (TRE) on glycolipid metabolism in adults compared to a control diet?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Adult study participants with details extracted from 23 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) totaling 1280 participants.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** 16/8 TRE dietary regimen involving a 16-hour fasting period and an 8-hour eating period.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison made to a control diet without specific time restrictions.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Fasting glucose, HOMA-IR, insulin levels, and HDL-C were the primary outcomes measured.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** 16/8 TRE resulted in slight reductions in fasting glucose, HOMA-IR, and insulin levels, and a modest effect on HDL-C. Subgroup analyses showed sex differences in TGs and LDL-C, with improvements in male participants and greater improvements in LDL-C with higher PA levels. Studies over 6 months showed significant HbA1c improvement.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study's limitations include variability in study durations, eating window lengths, and population characteristics which may affect the generalizability of the findings.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes or metabolic risk, the 16/8 TRE may offer some improvements in metabolic markers, but the effects can be influenced by personal factors such as sex, physical activity, and intervention duration. It's important to consider these factors when discussing dietary strategies with a healthcare provider.

## English Plain-Language Version

Starting the day with breakfast and ending meals by early evening might benefit blood sugar levels. A systematic review indicates this 16/8 TRE pattern slightly lowers fasting glucose and insulin resistance.

It also improves 'good' cholesterol, HDL-C. Effects vary; men and those more active see greater benefits in triglycerides and 'bad' cholesterol, LDL-C. Longer studies show better blood sugar control over time.

It's not a universal fix but could be part of a discussion with healthcare providers.

Balancing diet within the 8-hour window supports the benefits seen in studies, suggesting more whole foods and fewer processed items. Finding a rhythm that fits daily life and metabolic health is key.

While promising, 16/8 TRE is part of a broader lifestyle including regular activity and a balanced diet. It's not a quick fix but a component of long-term management for prediabetes and metabolic health.

In conclusion, 16/8 TRE could be a useful tool in managing metabolic health, offering a structured approach to eating that may help with blood sugar and cholesterol levels.

However, it's wise to consider overall lifestyle and work with healthcare professionals to find the best plan.

Managing prediabetes and metabolic risk involves more than just medication; lifestyle changes, particularly diet, play a crucial role.
