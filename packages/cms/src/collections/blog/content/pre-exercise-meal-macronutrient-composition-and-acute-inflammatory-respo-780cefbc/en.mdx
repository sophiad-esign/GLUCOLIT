## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How does the macronutrient composition of a pre-exercise meal affect the acute inflammatory response to exercise in male adults with type 2 diabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Ten males with well-controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Five moderate-intensity exercise sessions under five different dietary conditions: fasting, and 2 hours after a meal enriched in 22g sucrose, 22g fructose, 15g fat, and aspartame.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of C-reactive protein (CRP) levels before, during, and after exercise under different dietary conditions.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** CRP levels remained unchanged during exercise and recovery periods in all conditions. CRP levels were higher in the fructose condition compared to sucrose and fasting conditions.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** The inflammatory response to exercise in males with T2DM may be independent of the macronutrient composition of the diet. A meal enriched in fructose could lead to an inflammatory response in these individuals.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study had a small sample size and only included males with well-controlled T2DM, limiting the generalizability of the results. The findings show an association but do not prove causation.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While the macronutrient composition of a pre-exercise meal may not significantly affect the inflammatory response during exercise in men with type 2 diabetes, fructose-rich meals could potentially increase inflammation. It's important to discuss pre-exercise meal planning with a healthcare provider to manage inflammation and support diabetes control.

实际步骤可能包括与你的医生讨论如何在运动前规划饮食，以最小化炎症并支持你的糖尿病管理。

## English Plain-Language Version

For men with type 2 diabetes, the meal before a workout might influence inflammation levels.

A study indicates that while the type of meal doesn't change inflammation during exercise, fructose-rich meals could spark an inflammatory response. Managing inflammation is key to controlling diabetes and preventing complications.

The research involved ten men with well-controlled diabetes who exercised under different dietary conditions. It was found that a fructose-rich meal could lead to higher inflammation compared to other types of meals.

Choosing the right foods to support health is essential. Always consult with a healthcare provider before making dietary changes.

The study suggests that the macronutrient composition of a pre-exercise meal may not significantly affect the inflammatory response during exercise in men with type 2 diabetes.

However, fructose appears to have a unique impact, potentially increasing inflammation. It's important to note that this study shows an association, not causation, and the sample size was small.

Practical steps could include discussing with your doctor how to structure your pre-exercise meals to minimize inflammation and support your diabetes management.

Understanding how diet and exercise interact is crucial for individuals with type 2 diabetes.

This study provides a starting point for conversations about meal planning before physical activity and highlights the need for more research on how different nutrients affect inflammation in various populations.
