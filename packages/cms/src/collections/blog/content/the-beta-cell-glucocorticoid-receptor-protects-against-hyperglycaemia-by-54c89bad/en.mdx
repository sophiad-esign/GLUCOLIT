## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How does disruption of glucocorticoid rhythmicity increase circulating insulin while maintaining glycaemic control, and is beta cell glucocorticoid receptor signalling required for this adaptive response?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Male C57BL/6J mice
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Flattening of glucocorticoid rhythms by subcutaneous corticosterone pellet implantation
- **对照 / Comparison:** Placebo-implanted controls and high-fat diet-fed mice
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Fasting plasma insulin and blood glucose levels, glucose-stimulated insulin secretion, and insulin tolerance tests
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Glucocorticoid rhythm flattening led to sustained hyperinsulinaemia with maintained euglycaemia. Beta cell-specific deletion of GR reduced the hyperinsulinaemic response and led to hyperglycaemia and impaired glucose tolerance.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was conducted in mice, and findings may not directly translate to humans. The sample size and specific conditions may limit the generalizability of the results.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While the study provides valuable insights into the role of glucocorticoid rhythms in glucose homeostasis, readers should be cautious about applying these findings directly to human conditions. It suggests the importance of considering sleep and stress management in the context of prediabetes but does not provide a direct treatment protocol.

## 原文精华摘要

想象一下，如果邻居很吵，我们很难睡个好觉。 同样，当我们身体的自然糖皮质激素节律被打乱时，我们的血糖水平也可能难以保持在健康状态。 最近在老鼠上的一项研究发现，打乱日常糖皮质激素节律可以导致胰岛素水平升高，而不影响血糖，就像吵闹的邻居让人不安，但不会引起明显的干扰。

这对于糖尿病前期患者尤其重要，因为它表明睡眠模式和压力管理可能在控制他们的病情中扮演关键角色。 该研究操纵了老鼠的糖皮质激素节律，并观察到它导致了胰岛素分泌的持续增加，这是糖尿病前期的一个关键因素。

研究还发现，缺乏β细胞中的糖皮质激素受体会减少高胰岛素反应，并导致血糖水平升高，表明这个受体在维持血糖稳态中的重要性。 虽然这项研究是在老鼠身上进行的，但其发现可能对人类有影响，特别是那些患有打乱皮质醇节律状况的人，如睡眠限制或轮班工作。

需要注意的是，这不是直接用于管理糖尿病前期的处方，而是进一步调查睡眠和压力对血糖调节作用的呼吁。 对于读者来说，实际步骤可能包括与他们的医疗服务提供者讨论睡眠模式和压力管理，因为这些可能是管理糖尿病前期的重要因素。

为什么和糖尿病前期有关？

睡眠和压力是影响许多人日常生活的常见问题。 了解这些因素如何影响血糖水平对于糖尿病前期患者至关重要，因为这可以帮助他们采取积极措施来管理自己的病情。 这项研究提供了联系睡眠模式、压力和血糖调节的机制的见解，这对于制定个性化的管理策略非常有价值。

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine trying to sleep with a noisy neighbor – it's hard to rest well. Similarly, when our body's natural glucocorticoid rhythm is disrupted, it can make it hard for our blood sugar to rest at a healthy level.

A recent study in mice found that disrupting the daily glucocorticoid rhythm can lead to high insulin levels without affecting blood sugar, much like a noisy neighbor causing restlessness without causing a visible disturbance.

This is particularly relevant for people with prediabetes, as it suggests that sleep patterns and stress management could play a crucial role in managing their condition.

The study manipulated the glucocorticoid rhythm in mice and observed that it led to a sustained increase in insulin secretion, which is a key factor in prediabetes.

It also found that the absence of the glucocorticoid receptor in beta cells reduced the hyperinsulinemic response and led to higher blood sugar levels, indicating the importance of this receptor in maintaining glucose homeostasis.

While the study was conducted in mice, its findings could have implications for humans, especially those with conditions that disrupt the cortisol rhythm, such as sleep restriction or shift work.

It's important to note that this is not a direct prescription for managing prediabetes but rather a call for further investigation into the role of sleep and stress on glucose regulation.

Practical steps for readers might include discussing sleep patterns and stress management with their healthcare provider, as these could be important factors in managing prediabetes.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Sleep and stress are common issues that can affect many people's daily lives.

Understanding how these factors can influence blood sugar levels is crucial for those with prediabetes, as it can help them take proactive steps to manage their condition.

This study provides insights into the mechanisms that link sleep patterns, stress, and blood sugar regulation, which can be valuable for developing personalized management strategies.

Practical Takeaways

- Disruptions in the body's glucocorticoid rhythm can lead to increased insulin levels, which is significant for those with prediabetes.
- Maintaining a healthy sleep pattern and managing stress could be crucial in managing prediabetes.
- The study in mice suggests that the glucocorticoid receptor in beta cells plays a key role in glucose homeostasis.
- Further investigation is needed to understand the role of sleep and stress on glucose regulation in humans.
- Discussing sleep patterns and stress management with a healthcare provider could be beneficial for those with prediabetes.
