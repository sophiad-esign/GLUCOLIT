- **研究问题 / Research question:** What are the key features of behavioral interventions that successfully improved diet and/or activity behaviors for type 2 diabetes prevention among adults in the United States?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Adults in the United States, particularly females, non-Hispanic White adults with at least some college education, aged 31 to 50 years.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Behavioral interventions focusing on diet and activity behaviors.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparisons were made between different intervention delivery modes and behavior change techniques.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Significant improvements in diet or activity behaviors among adults.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Most efficacious interventions were individual-based, used a mix of delivery modes, and included common behavior change techniques such as goal setting, instruction, social support, and feedback.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're at a crossroads where one path leads to better health and the other to diabetes. The right turn is taking steps to improve your diet and exercise habits.

According to a scoping review, these are key to preventing type 2 diabetes. The review looked at studies that showed significant improvements in diet or activity behaviors among adults.

Most successful interventions focused on women, non-Hispanic White adults with some college education, aged 31 to 50. They used a mix of face-to-face, electronic, and environmental approaches.

Common techniques included setting goals, instructions, social support, and feedback. While these methods worked, they need to be tested in more diverse groups.

For you, this means that improving diet and activity could be your best bet against diabetes, but remember, what works for one might not work for all.

It's about finding what fits your lifestyle and discussing it with your healthcare provider.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Type 2 diabetes is a growing health concern, and understanding how to prevent it is crucial. Exercise and diet are two areas where individuals can take action to improve their health outcomes.

Practical Takeaways

- Improving diet and physical activity can significantly reduce the risk of type 2 diabetes.
- Behavioral interventions should be tailored to individual needs and lifestyles.
- Goal setting, social support, and feedback are effective techniques in behavior change.
- Diverse populations should be considered in future research to ensure interventions are widely applicable.
