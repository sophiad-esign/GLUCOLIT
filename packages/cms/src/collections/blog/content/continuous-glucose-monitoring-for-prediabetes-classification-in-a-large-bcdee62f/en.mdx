## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Can continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) metrics classify prediabetes with performance approaching that of HbA1c and FPG?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 1,883 participants from the Human Phenotype Project with concurrent CGM, HbA1c, and FPG data.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Evaluation of individual CGM metrics across four ADA-based laboratory reference definitions and a combined model incorporating six clinically selected CGM metrics.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of CGM metrics to HbA1c and FPG for prediabetes classification.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Discrimination quantified using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC).
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** HbA1c- and FPG-based classifications showed marked discordance, with only 9.5% of participants meeting both prediabetes criteria. Several CGM metrics demonstrated classification performance approaching that of laboratory markers (AUC range 0.645-0.677).
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was cross-sectional and did not include oral glucose tolerance testing or longitudinal outcomes.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While CGM shows promise as a complementary tool to traditional methods for classifying prediabetes, it should not replace them but rather be used in conjunction to provide a more comprehensive understanding of an individual's metabolic health.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine waking up and checking a device that tells you how your body is handling sugar overnight. This is the promise of continuous glucose monitoring (CGM), which could help identify prediabetes more accurately.

Traditional methods like HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose (FPG) sometimes give conflicting results, which can be confusing for those trying to understand their risk of developing diabetes.

A recent study evaluated whether CGM could offer a clearer picture. The study analyzed data from nearly 2,000 participants, comparing CGM metrics to the established HbA1c and FPG methods.

While no single CGM metric outperformed the traditional methods, some came close, suggesting that CGM could be a useful tool when traditional methods yield conflicting results.

It's important to note that CGM is not a standalone solution but rather a complementary approach to help manage and understand prediabetes.

The study's findings are a reminder that managing prediabetes is not a one-size-fits-all approach. It suggests that a combination of methods, including CGM, may provide a more comprehensive understanding of an individual's metabolic health.

This could lead to more personalized and effective interventions. However, it's crucial to discuss any new monitoring methods with a healthcare provider to ensure they are appropriate for your specific health needs.

In practical terms, understanding your prediabetes risk means being proactive about your health. This might involve regular check-ups, dietary adjustments, and physical activity.

It's about working with your healthcare team to find the best approach for you. Remember, the goal is not just to monitor your glucose levels but to take actionable steps towards better health.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the nuances of prediabetes is crucial for early intervention and management. CGM offers a potential new tool in this space, which could lead to more personalized and effective care strategies.

Practical Takeaways

- CGM can provide a complementary approach to traditional methods for identifying prediabetes.
- No single CGM metric outperformed traditional methods, but some were close, suggesting potential value when traditional methods are discordant.
- Combining different methods may offer a more comprehensive understanding of an individual's metabolic health.
- It's important to discuss new monitoring methods with a healthcare provider to ensure they are appropriate for your specific health needs.
- Understanding prediabetes risk involves being proactive about health through regular check-ups, dietary adjustments, and physical activity.
