## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the HOMA-IR cut-off point associated with metabolic syndrome in Peruvian adolescents, and what are the modifiable risk factors?
- **研究对象 / Population:** A sample of 371 Peruvian adolescents from low- to medium-socioeconomic backgrounds.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Secondary data analysis of a longitudinal study assessing insulin resistance and metabolic risk factors.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of HOMA-IR values with metabolic syndrome criteria and assessment of lifestyle factors.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** HOMA-IR values, presence of insulin resistance, and associated cardiometabolic risk factors.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** A HOMA-IR cut-off of 3.9 was identified for predicting metabolic syndrome with 72.4% sensitivity and 75.4% specificity. 28.6% of adolescents had insulin resistance.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study did not find associations between diet, excess weight in infancy, and family history of non-chronic disease with insulin resistance.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While diet and physical activity are important for managing insulin resistance, the specific factors influencing it can vary, and individual risk may not be fully explained by these factors alone.

## English Plain-Language Version

Feeling sluggish after a heavy meal? It might indicate insulin resistance, a precursor to diabetes. A Peruvian study identified a HOMA-IR score of 3. 9 as signaling high metabolic risk.

A diet rich in whole foods and regular physical activity can help lower this risk. A balanced diet with portion control can manage insulin resistance. Regular exercise, even a few times a week, improves insulin sensitivity.

The study showed 84% of teens had at least one cardiometabolic risk factor. The study did not find a direct link with diet or early infancy weight and insulin resistance. Instead of focusing solely on diet, consider how much you move.

A combination of a balanced diet and regular physical activity is key.

GLUCOLIT insight: For adolescents, creating a supportive environment that encourages healthy eating and active lifestyles can be as important as the diet and exercise themselves. Schools and communities can play a significant role in this.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Insulin resistance is a key factor in the development of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

Understanding how diet and exercise can influence insulin resistance is crucial for preventing these chronic conditions, especially in high-risk groups like adolescents.

Practical Takeaways

- A HOMA-IR score of 3.9 or higher may indicate high metabolic risk in adolescents.
- Diet and regular physical activity are key to managing insulin resistance.
- Small, sustainable changes in diet and exercise can have a big impact on health.
- Discuss lifestyle changes with your doctor to manage prediabetes and metabolic risk.
- Community and school support can be crucial for encouraging healthy habits in adolescents.
