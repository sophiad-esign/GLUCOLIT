## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the impact of long-distance running on individuals with diabetes, prediabetes, or metabolic conditions?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Runners with confirmed diabetes mellitus participating in endurance events, and a large cohort of male and female participants from the National Runners’ Health Study.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Participation in long-distance running events, including marathons, and the use of continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) for glycemic control.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of medication use for hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, and diabetes among marathon participants versus nonparticipants.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Prevalence of hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, and diabetes; time-in-range for glucose levels; incidence of hypoglycemia.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Marathon participation was associated with a lower prevalence of hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, and diabetes, independent of annual running distance. Time-in-range for glucose levels varied by race distance, and asymptomatic late-onset hypoglycemia was reported in some runners.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The studies reviewed had varying methodologies and populations, and the cross-sectional nature of the study limits causal inference.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While evidence suggests that long-distance running may have beneficial effects on metabolic health, it's important to consider individual differences and discuss exercise plans with healthcare providers to ensure safety and effectiveness.

## English Plain-Language Version

Running long distances can be a powerful tool for managing prediabetes and improving metabolic health. Imagine a runner with prediabetes; by lacing up for a marathon, they may reduce their need for medication.

Studies show that the more marathons a person runs, the less likely they are to need drugs for hypertension, high cholesterol, and diabetes.

This doesn't mean everyone should start training for a marathon, but it does suggest that regular, long-distance running could be part of a healthy lifestyle to manage these conditions.

It's important to prepare properly and consider factors like frequency, intensity, and safety to make running a sustainable and beneficial part of your routine.

While the evidence is promising, it's also important to understand the limits. Not all studies have the same results, and what works for one person might not work for another.

It's crucial to discuss any significant changes in exercise routine with a healthcare provider to ensure it's safe and appropriate.

In practical terms, this means incorporating long runs into your weekly schedule, starting gradually, and listening to your body. It might also mean adjusting your diet to support your new exercise regimen.

Remember, the goal is not just to run, but to improve overall health and well-being.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Exercise, particularly long-distance running, is a key component in managing prediabetes and metabolic health. Understanding how it affects these conditions can help individuals make informed decisions about their health.

Practical Takeaways

- Long-distance running can be a part of a healthy lifestyle to manage prediabetes and metabolic conditions.
- The frequency of marathon participation is associated with a lower need for certain medications.
- It's important to prepare properly and consider safety when incorporating long runs into your routine.
- Discuss any significant changes in exercise routine with a healthcare provider.
- Incorporate long runs gradually and listen to your body to ensure sustainability and benefit.
