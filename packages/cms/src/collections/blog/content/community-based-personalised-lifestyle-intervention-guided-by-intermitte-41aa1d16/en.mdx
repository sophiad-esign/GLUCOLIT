## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Does a community-based personalized lifestyle intervention guided by intermittent continuous glucose monitoring improve glycaemic and cardiometabolic outcomes in adults with type 2 diabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 180 adults with type 2 diabetes and pre-randomisation HbA1c > 7.0%
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** CGM-guided intervention with FreeStyle Libre 2 use and personalized lifestyle guidance, including supervised exercise in the first 3 months
- **对照 / Comparison:** Usual community diabetes management
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Primary outcome was HbA1c at Month 3
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** No significant difference in HbA1c between the intervention and control groups at Month 3, 6, or 12
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study did not find a sustained benefit from the intervention, and the sample size may limit the ability to detect smaller effects
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While the intervention did not significantly improve outcomes beyond usual care, it highlights the need for further research into how to effectively integrate lifestyle changes, including exercise, into diabetes management.

## 原文精华摘要

想象一下，你正在管理自己的血糖水平，而一种新的方法结合了监测和个性化建议以及运动。 最近的一项研究调查了这种方法，但发现虽然这个想法很有前景，但结果并不像预期的那样有影响力。 这项研究涉及2型糖尿病患者，他们得到了一种特殊的血糖监测器和生活方式指导，以及在前三个月的监督运动。

三个月后，他们的血糖控制有了轻微的、非显著的改善。 然而，这种改善并没有持续下去，到了年底，这个群体和那些接受标准护理的人之间没有差异。 看来，尽管运动和监测是好的，但还需要更多的研究来找出如何使这种方法真正奏效。

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're managing your blood sugar levels, and a new approach combines monitoring with personalized advice and exercise.

A recent study looked into this but found that while the idea is promising, the results were not as impactful as hoped.

The study involved adults with type 2 diabetes who were given a special glucose monitor and lifestyle guidance, along with supervised exercise in the first three months.

After three months, there was a slight, non-significant improvement in their blood sugar control. However, this didn't last, and by the end of the year, there was no difference between this group and those who received standard care.

It seems that while exercise and monitoring are good, more research is needed to find out how to make this approach really work.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Exercise is a critical component of managing diabetes, and understanding how it can be effectively integrated into lifestyle interventions is important for people with prediabetes or early metabolic risk.

Practical Takeaways

- Personalized lifestyle interventions including exercise may offer some short-term benefits for blood sugar control.
- Long-term effects of such interventions were not significantly different from standard care.
- More research is needed to understand how to make lifestyle interventions more effective.
- It's important to discuss any new treatment approaches with healthcare providers.
