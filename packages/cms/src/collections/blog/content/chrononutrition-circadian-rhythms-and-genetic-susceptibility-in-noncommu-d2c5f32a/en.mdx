## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How do sleep quality, chrononutrition, and circadian rhythms interact to influence metabolic and cardiovascular diseases, particularly in the context of sex-specific differences?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Individuals with prediabetes, insulin resistance, or early metabolic risk.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Sleep deprivation, circadian misalignment, and chrononutrition strategies.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Associations between sleep quality, hormonal pathways, and metabolic health outcomes.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Insulin resistance, appetite dysregulation, elevated blood pressure, and cardiometabolic risk.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Sleep deprivation and circadian misalignment are associated with insulin resistance and increased cardiometabolic risk. Chrononutrition strategies show beneficial effects on sleep quality, glycemic control, inflammation, and metabolic health.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is a narrative review and does not establish causation. Individual responses to sleep and dietary interventions may vary.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While the evidence suggests associations between sleep, chrononutrition, and metabolic health, readers should consult with healthcare providers to discuss personalized strategies and not overclaim the findings as causation.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine waking up refreshed and ready to tackle the day, knowing that your sleep and diet are working in harmony to support your metabolic health.

Recent research highlights the significant role that sleep quality and chrononutrition play in managing prediabetes and insulin resistance.

Studies consistently show that sleep deprivation and circadian misalignment are linked to insulin resistance and increased cardiometabolic risk.

Hormonal pathways, including those involving leptin, ghrelin, cortisol, and melatonin, mediate these effects.

Practical steps include establishing a regular sleep schedule, aiming for 7-9 hours of sleep per night, and considering chrononutrition strategies like time-restricted eating.

It's important to note that these are associations and not direct causes, and individual responses may vary. Integrating these strategies into your lifestyle can be a proactive step towards better metabolic health.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Sleep and diet are integral to managing metabolic health, especially for those with prediabetes or early metabolic risk. Understanding the connection between these factors can empower individuals to make informed lifestyle choices.

Practical Takeaways

- Establish a regular sleep schedule to support your metabolic health.
- Aim for 7-9 hours of sleep per night to help manage insulin resistance.
- Consider chrononutrition strategies like time-restricted eating for better sleep quality and glycemic control.
- Consult with a healthcare provider to discuss personalized strategies based on your genetic predisposition and lifestyle.
