## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How do different insulin resistance indices predict mortality in hypertensive adults across various glycemic states?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 7,548 hypertensive adults from NHANES 1999-2018, classified as normoglycemia, prediabetes, and diabetes.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Evaluation of ten insulin resistance indices including HOMA-IR, TyG, and eGDR.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of the predictive value of fasting-insulin-based versus non-insulin-based indices for all-cause and cardiovascular mortality.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** All-cause mortality (ACM) and cardiovascular mortality (CVM).
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** HOMA-IR was the only fasting-insulin-based index associated with ACM across all glycemic states. eGDR showed the strongest associations with ACM and CVM, especially in prediabetes and diabetes. TyG was significantly associated with ACM and CVM, particularly in diabetes.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is observational and does not establish causation. The findings are specific to hypertensive adults and may not be generalizable to other populations.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For hypertensive adults, the choice of insulin resistance index can impact mortality predictions, suggesting a personalized approach based on glycemic state may be beneficial.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're at a crossroads, unsure which path to take. This is similar to what doctors face when choosing an insulin resistance index for hypertensive patients. A new study sheds light on which index might be the best fit.

Insulin resistance is linked to hypertension and can predict mortality. Researchers evaluated ten indices, including HOMA-IR, TyG, and eGDR, across different glycemic states.

They found HOMA-IR was the only fasting-insulin-based index associated with all-cause mortality across all glycemic states.

eGDR showed the strongest associations with all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, especially in prediabetes and diabetes. TyG was also significant, particularly in diabetes.

This means for hypertensive adults, choosing the right index based on their glycemic state could improve mortality predictions. It's not a one-size-fits-all approach; it's about personalization.

Create a routine that works for you, and if you consistently wake up feeling unrested, it might be a sign of a sleep disorder.

Remember, these are not medical prescriptions but lifestyle adjustments that can be discussed with your healthcare provider. Personalize your approach based on your glycemic state and overall health.
