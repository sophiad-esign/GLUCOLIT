## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How does a night shift-like schedule affect glucose metabolism in reproductive-aged women?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 52 women with HbA1c <5.7%
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** One night of sleep and one night of wakefulness in a laboratory environment
- **对照 / Comparison:** Fasting and postprandial glucose metabolism after sleep vs. wakefulness
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Fasting glucose, HOMA-IR, LAR, post-OGTT blood glucose levels, and respiratory quotient (RQ)
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Post-OGTT blood glucose at 60 minutes and peak glucose were higher following the night shift compared with sleep. The P/E ratio was not significantly associated with fasting or postprandial glucose. Pre-OGTT RQ was lower after the night shift.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study sample was limited to women, and the effects of a single night of wakefulness may not extrapolate to chronic sleep deprivation.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For women with prediabetes, a night of wakefulness can impair glucose tolerance the next morning, suggesting the importance of diet choices after sleep deprivation and the potential impact of sleep on glucose metabolism independent of ovarian hormones.

## English Plain-Language Version

After a night shift, you might feel the urge for breakfast. But opting for low-glycemic index foods could help manage blood sugar levels better.

According to a study, a night of wakefulness can impair glucose tolerance the next morning, regardless of hormone levels. This is significant for women with prediabetes.

The study involved 52 women who spent one night sleeping and another night staying awake in a lab.

They found that after a night shift, blood glucose levels were higher 60 minutes post an oral glucose tolerance test and at peak times compared to after a night of sleep.

Interestingly, the ratio of progesterone to estradiol did not affect glucose levels, indicating that the impact of sleep on glucose is not through these hormones.

The body's reliance on fat oxidation versus carbohydrate utilization, measured by respiratory quotient (RQ), was also lower after a night shift, suggesting that the body's metabolic processes are altered by sleep deprivation.

For women with prediabetes who work night shifts, choosing low-glycemic index foods for breakfast might help in managing postprandial glucose spikes.

It's also crucial to maintain a consistent sleep schedule and avoid strenuous physical activity before bedtime to ensure better sleep quality.

While this study provides insights, it's not a prescription for health. Always consult with a healthcare provider before making significant changes to your diet or sleep routine.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Sleep quality and diet are critical factors in managing prediabetes, especially for those with irregular work hours like night shifts.

Understanding how sleep affects glucose metabolism can help individuals make informed decisions about their diet and lifestyle.

Practical Takeaways

- A night of wakefulness can impair glucose tolerance the next morning, affecting women with prediabetes.
- Choosing low-glycemic index foods for the first meal after a night shift may help manage blood sugar levels.
- It's important to maintain a consistent sleep schedule and avoid strenuous physical activity before bedtime.
- The impact of sleep on glucose metabolism is not through ovarian hormones like progesterone and estradiol.
- Always consult with a healthcare provider before making significant changes to your diet or sleep routine.
