## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Can lifestyle changes in individuals with prediabetes lead to cognitive improvements?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Participants with coronary heart disease and prediabetes (HbA1c of 5.7% to 6.4%) in Canada.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** A 6-month multidisciplinary enhanced cardiac rehabilitation intervention focusing on physical activity, nutrition, and education.
- **对照 / Comparison:** No control group; used standardized regression-based formulas and reliable change indexes to account for practice effects.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Significant improvements in global cognition, memory, processing speed, and executive function.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Participants showed cognitive improvements beyond expected practice effects, with weight loss and reductions in HbA1c linked to these gains.
- **局限性 / Limits:** Observational study without a control group; causality cannot be established.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While the study suggests potential cognitive benefits from prediabetes remission, further research is needed to confirm these findings and explore the mechanisms behind them.

## English Plain-Language Version

Managing health can lead to sharper cognitive skills. A Canadian study found that individuals with prediabetes who changed their lifestyle improved not just their blood sugar levels but also their cognitive abilities.

Participants with prediabetes and coronary heart disease completed a six-month rehabilitation program, showing enhancements in cognition, memory, speed, and executive functions.

The cognitive improvements were notable, with some exceeding expected practice effects, indicating a real impact of the intervention. Weight loss and HbA1c reductions were associated with these cognitive gains.

While the study is observational and without a control group, its findings suggest that addressing prediabetes could have benefits beyond physical health.

The study offers a dual incentive for prediabetes action: better metabolic health and potential cognitive skill enhancement. It underscores the interconnection of body and brain health.

The findings call for further research and provide practical guidance for a comprehensive approach to prediabetes management, including physical activity, nutrition, and education.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

This study is relevant because it highlights the potential cognitive benefits of managing prediabetes, a condition that affects millions and is often overlooked in terms of its broader health implications.

Practical Takeaways

- Reversing prediabetes may lead to significant cognitive improvements.
- Weight loss and reductions in HbA1c are linked to cognitive gains.
- A comprehensive approach to prediabetes management can improve both physical and cognitive health.
- The study suggests a double incentive for individuals with prediabetes to take action.
- Further research is needed to confirm the cognitive benefits of prediabetes remission.
