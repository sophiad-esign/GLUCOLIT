## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What are the independent clinical and biochemical risk factors for prediabetes, and how can they be used to construct a predictive model for early diabetes screening?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 2,376 adult participants, including 1,242 patients with prediabetes and 1,134 normoglycemic controls.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Retrospective cross-sectional analysis of clinical registry data.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of demographic, anthropometric, and biochemical profiles between prediabetes patients and normoglycemic controls.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Identification of independent risk and protective factors using multivariable logistic regression and evaluation of the discriminative capacity through ROC curve analysis.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Gender, age, BMI, MPV, total protein, γ-GT, UA, and apolipoprotein A1 were identified as independent risk factors, while serum creatinine and HDL-C were protective factors. An 8-parameter model achieved a bias-corrected AUC of 0.806, indicating good discriminative ability.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study's retrospective cross-sectional design limits causal inference. The generalizability of the findings may be limited to similar populations and settings.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** This study highlights the importance of a multivariable approach to prediabetes risk assessment, including dietary factors. It suggests that integrating routine biomarkers into a predictive model can aid in early diabetes screening and emphasizes the role of diet in managing prediabetes.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're at a crossroads, where one path leads to type 2 diabetes and the other leads back to health. The right diet can guide you on the path to better health.

A recent study identified key risk factors for prediabetes, including age, BMI, and certain blood markers. These findings underscore the importance of a balanced diet in managing these risks.

The study showed that while individual factors had limited predictive power, a combination of factors, including dietary factors, could significantly improve the ability to predict prediabetes.

This suggests that a multifaceted approach, including diet, is crucial in diabetes prevention.

To apply this to your meals, consider swapping high-glycemic foods like white bread for whole grains, and include a variety of colorful vegetables to ensure a range of nutrients. Portion control is also key;

aim for balanced plates with a focus on lean proteins and plenty of fiber from vegetables and whole grains. Remember, the goal is not to completely deprive yourself but to make informed choices that support your health.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the role of diet in prediabetes is crucial because it can significantly influence the progression to type 2 diabetes.

Making dietary changes is one of the most accessible and effective ways to manage prediabetes and prevent the onset of diabetes.

Practical Takeaways

- A balanced diet is key in managing prediabetes.
- Swap high-glycemic foods for whole grains and include a variety of vegetables.
- Portion control is essential; aim for balanced plates with lean proteins and plenty of fiber.
- Making informed food choices can support your health journey.
- Discuss dietary changes with a healthcare provider to find a plan that works for you.
