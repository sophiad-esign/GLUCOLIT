## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How does exercise reshape the gut microbiota to improve glycolipid metabolism and alleviate obesity?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Obese mice
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Exercise
- **对照 / Comparison:** Non-exercised control group
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Increased abundance of beneficial bacteria, increased medium- and long-chain fatty acid content, decreased d-fructose levels, improved insulin sensitivity, and improved glucose homeostasis
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Exercise reshaped the gut microbiota to promote the production of medium- and long-chain fatty acids, thereby upregulating GLP-1 secretion, and ultimately improving glycolipid metabolism and alleviating obesity.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was conducted in mice, and while the findings are promising, direct applicability to humans is not confirmed. Exercise effects can vary among individuals.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes or early metabolic risk, regular exercise may not only help with weight management but also improve metabolic health by reshaping the gut microbiota. However, the specific mechanisms and effects in humans need further investigation, and individual responses to exercise can vary.

## English Plain-Language Version

Gut microbiota, the microorganisms in our intestines, significantly influence metabolism and weight. Exercise, known for combating obesity, can also alter this microbial community to boost health.

A study illuminates how exercise might aid by increasing medium and long-chain fatty acids, linked to better fat breakdown and insulin sensitivity.

This was demonstrated in obese mice using a multi-omics approach, including fecal microbiota transplantation.

The study hints at the potential for exercise to adjust our gut microbiota, aiding in weight and blood sugar management. While focused on mice, the findings are promising for human health. Exercise isn't a universal remedy;

individual responses vary. However, it suggests considering exercise in a broader strategy to manage prediabetes and early metabolic risks. It's about fostering a healthy gut environment that supports metabolic health.

Exercise isn't a quick fix; consistency and gradual progression are crucial for long-term benefits. Finding a sustainable balance is key, whether through daily walks, weekly swims, or a mix of enjoyable activities.

Consulting healthcare providers before starting new exercise regimens is advisable, especially with existing health conditions.

In summary, exercise's power extends beyond calories burned. It can reshape our gut microbiota, potentially enhancing metabolic health.

Yet, it's part of a larger puzzle that includes a balanced diet, regular exercise, and good sleep for managing prediabetes and early metabolic risks.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the interaction between exercise and gut microbiota provides insights into how physical activity can influence metabolic health beyond burning calories, which is particularly relevant for individuals with prediabetes or early

metabolic risk.

Practical Takeaways

- Exercise can reshape the gut microbiota to improve glycolipid metabolism.
- Regular exercise may help manage weight and blood sugar levels more effectively.
- A balanced diet, regular exercise, and good sleep are all part of a healthy lifestyle that can help manage prediabetes and early metabolic risks.
- Exercise isn't a one-size-fits-all prescription; find a balance that works for you.
- Consult with a healthcare provider before starting any new exercise regimen, especially if you have existing health conditions.
