## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How does simulated shift work affect postprandial glucose and insulin responses, and lipid rhythms?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Nine healthy non-shift-worker participants (age 21-33 years, 3 females)
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Simulated shift work intervention with four consecutive meals at the same clock times during day and night shifts
- **对照 / Comparison:** Day-shift followed by two night-shifts of 10-h delayed sleep and mealtimes
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Plasma glucose, insulin, triglycerides, HDL-cholesterol, total cholesterol measured hourly; continuous interstitial glucose monitoring; subjective hunger and appetite assessed
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Lipid rhythms shifted with 10-h delay in sleep-wake times; night-shifts elevated postprandial glucose and insulin; time-of-day effects persisted irrespective of shift with 18:00 meals producing the highest glycaemic responses
- **局限性 / Limits:** Small sample size; short duration of the study; all participants were healthy non-shift workers
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While this study shows associations between shift work, meal timing, and metabolic responses, it does not establish causation. It suggests that for individuals with prediabetes or insulin resistance, managing meal and sleep timing could be beneficial for metabolic health.

这项研究涉及健康的个体，他们经历了一个模拟轮班工作协议，在同一时间钟点吃相同的食物，但在白天和夜晚轮班。 他们发现，随着睡眠-觉醒时间的延迟，体内的脂质节律显著变化，夜班导致餐后血糖和胰岛素水平更高。 这表明，饮食和睡眠的时机对于维持代谢健康至关重要。

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine trying to fit a square peg into a round hole—it just doesn’t work. Similarly, our bodies have natural rhythms that, when disrupted, can lead to metabolic issues.

A recent study sheds light on how shift work, which disrupts our circadian rhythms, can affect our body's response to food and sleep.

The study involved healthy individuals who underwent a simulated shift work protocol, consuming identical meals at the same clock times but during day and night shifts.

What they found was that lipid rhythms in the body shifted significantly with the delay in sleep-wake times, and night shifts led to higher postprandial glucose and insulin levels.

This suggests that the timing of meals and sleep is crucial for maintaining metabolic health. It's not just about what you eat, but also when you eat and sleep.

For those with prediabetes or insulin resistance, understanding these rhythms can help in managing blood sugar levels and overall health.

It's important to note that while this study provides insights, it doesn't mean that shift work directly causes metabolic diseases. However, it does highlight the importance of considering meal and sleep timing in managing metabolic health.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Shift work and irregular meal times are common in modern life, and this study provides insights into how these factors can affect metabolic health, which is particularly relevant for individuals with prediabetes or insulin resistance.

Practical Takeaways

- Maintaining a consistent sleep schedule is crucial for metabolic health.
- Meal timing can significantly impact your body's response to food.
- Night shifts may disrupt lipid rhythms and increase postprandial glucose and insulin levels.
- Understanding your body's natural rhythms can help manage prediabetes and insulin resistance.
