## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the effect of intensive lifestyle intervention, with or without bariatric surgery, on urine albumin-creatinine ratio (UACR) in adolescents with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Adolescents aged 15-17 years with BMI ≥ 35 kg/m2 in New Zealand, identified as Māori or Pacific, with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** A 12-week very-low-calorie diet (VLCD) program followed by an offer of bariatric surgery for those with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes identified through HbA1c screening.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of UACR trajectories between surgical and non-surgical participants over 36 months.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Urine albumin-creatinine ratio (UACR) measured at up to 14 time points over 36 months.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** A significant time × surgery interaction was observed (p < 0.001). Surgical participants had an 86% reduction in UACR at 36 months, while non-surgical participants showed no significant change.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study had a small sample size and focused on a specific demographic, limiting generalizability. The study's duration was 36 months, which may not capture long-term effects.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For adolescents with prediabetes or early type 2 diabetes, particularly those from high-risk populations, an intensive lifestyle intervention combined with bariatric surgery may significantly reduce albuminuria, suggesting potential renoprotective effects. However, the small sample size and specific population limit the applicability of these findings to broader groups.

## English Plain-Language Version

Facing weight and health challenges, adolescents now have a promising approach. A study shows that combining diet changes with surgery can significantly reduce albuminuria, a key marker of kidney health.

This is especially relevant for Māori and Pacific youth in New Zealand, who often grapple with severe obesity.

The research involved a 12-week very-low-calorie diet program, followed by an offer of bariatric surgery for those identified with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes through HbA1c screening.

Over 36 months, surgical participants experienced an 86% reduction in albuminuria, a notable improvement over the non-surgical group.

This indicates that for certain adolescents, lifestyle changes paired with surgery could be a potent strategy for kidney health protection.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Adolescents with prediabetes or early type 2 diabetes are at a pivotal point where lifestyle changes can greatly affect their long-term health.

Understanding the potential benefits of intensive lifestyle interventions, including diet and surgery, is essential for managing and potentially reversing these conditions.

Practical Takeaways

- A 12-week very-low-calorie diet can initiate prediabetes management in adolescents.
- Bariatric surgery combined with lifestyle changes may offer significant renal benefits.
- Surgical participants saw an 86% reduction in albuminuria over 36 months.
- Individualized lifestyle interventions should be discussed with healthcare providers.
