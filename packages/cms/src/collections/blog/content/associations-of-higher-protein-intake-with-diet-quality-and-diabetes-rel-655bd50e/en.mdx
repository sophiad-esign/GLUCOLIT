## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the association between protein intake, diet quality, and diabetes-related metabolic health indicators in Korean adults without diabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 8,254 adults aged 19-64 years without diabetes from the Korean National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey 2019-2021, stratified by sex and pre-diabetic status.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Protein intake assessed using a 24-hour recall, expressed as a percentage of total energy intake, and divided into quintiles.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Diet quality examined using the Korean Healthy Eating Index; diabetes-related metabolic indicators included waist circumference, triglyceride, HDL-C, blood pressure, HOMA-IR, and triglyceride-glucose index.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Positive association between protein intake and diet quality, with a peak at Q4 then a decline; pre-diabetic men showed a decreasing trend in elevated triglyceride risk with higher protein intake; women showed an increased risk of low HDL-C with higher protein intake.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Appropriate, balanced protein intake may help manage metabolic alterations associated with diabetes in Korean adults without diabetes.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study shows association, not causation; results varied by sex; potential for confounding factors.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While higher protein intake is linked to better diet quality, it's important to find a balance that works for individual metabolic health, considering the potential risks and benefits.

## 原文精华摘要

晚宴上摆满了各种菜肴，富含蛋白质的肉类可能很诱人，但研究表明平衡才是关键。 在韩国成年非糖尿病患者中，较高的蛋白质摄入与更好的饮食质量相关，但这种好处在达到一定水平后开始下降。

对于有糖尿病前期的男性来说，较高的蛋白质摄入与降低高甘油三酯风险相关，而女性摄入较高蛋白质则与低高密度脂蛋白胆固醇风险增加相关。 这些发现强调了平衡饮食的重要性，而不是简单地增加蛋白质摄入。

蛋白质对我们的身体至关重要，但多少才算过多？ 这项研究分析了8000多名韩国成年人的数据，观察他们的蛋白质摄入占总能量摄入的百分比。 他们发现，虽然较高的蛋白质摄入与更好的饮食质量相关，但好处在达到一定水平后开始下降。 这表明存在一个蛋白质摄入的“甜蜜点”，可以帮助管理代谢健康。

需要注意的是，这项研究并没有证明因果关系； 它只显示了一种关联。 结果在男性和女性之间也有所不同，表明蛋白质摄入的效果可能因性别而异。 虽然较高的蛋白质摄入与一些好处相关，但也显示出潜在的风险，特别是对女性的高密度脂蛋白胆固醇水平。

如果您希望管理自己的代谢健康，可能值得与医疗保健提供者讨论您的蛋白质摄入。 他们可以帮助您找到适合您身体的平衡点。 并且记住，平衡饮食不仅仅是蛋白质——它还涉及到从各种食物中获取各种营养素。

总之，虽然蛋白质是健康饮食的重要组成部分，但这项研究提醒我们，适度和平衡是关键。 这不仅仅是关于你吃多少蛋白质，还涉及到你整体饮食的质量。

为什么和糖尿病前期有关？

这项研究之所以相关，是因为它提供了关于蛋白质摄入这一许多饮食中的关键组成部分可能如何影响成年非糖尿病患者的代谢健康，特别是在糖尿病前期的背景下的见解。

- 有糖尿病前期的男性可能会看到随着蛋白质摄入的增加，高甘油三酯的风险降低。

## English Plain-Language Version

Dinner parties offer a variety of dishes. While protein-rich meats might be tempting, a study suggests balance is key.

In Korean adults without diabetes, higher protein intake is linked to better diet quality, but this benefit peaks and then declines. For men with pre-diabetes, higher protein intake is associated with a lower risk of elevated triglycerides.

In contrast, women with higher protein intake face an increased risk of low HDL cholesterol. These findings highlight the importance of a balanced diet over simply increasing protein.

Protein is essential, but how much is too much? The study analyzed data from over 8,000 Korean adults, looking at their protein intake as a percentage of total energy intake.

It found that while higher protein intake is linked to better diet quality, the benefits start to decline after a certain point. This indicates there's an optimal level of protein intake that can help manage metabolic health.

It's important to note that this study doesn't prove causation; it only shows an association. The results also varied between men and women, indicating that the effects of protein intake might differ based on sex.

And while higher protein intake is linked to some benefits, it also showed potential risks, particularly for women and their HDL cholesterol levels.

If you're looking to manage your metabolic health, discussing your protein intake with a healthcare provider might be beneficial. They can help you find the right balance that works for your body.

Remember, a balanced diet is about more than just protein—it's about getting a variety of nutrients from a range of foods.

In conclusion, while protein is an essential part of a healthy diet, this study reminds us that moderation and balance are key. It's not just about how much protein you eat, but also the quality of your overall diet.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

This study is relevant because it provides insights into how protein intake, a key component of many diets, may affect metabolic health in adults without diabetes, particularly in the context of pre-diabetes.

Practical Takeaways

- Protein intake should be balanced rather than simply increased for better metabolic health.
- The benefits of higher protein intake may peak and then decline, suggesting a 'sweet spot' for protein consumption.
- Men with pre-diabetes may see a reduced risk of elevated triglycerides with higher protein intake.
- Women may face an increased risk of low HDL cholesterol with higher protein intake.
- A balanced diet with a variety of nutrients is key to managing metabolic health.
