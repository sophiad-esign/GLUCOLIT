## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're at a crossroads with two paths: one leading to a pro-inflammatory diet and the other to regular physical activity. The question is, can the latter mitigate the health risks associated with the former?

This narrative review suggests that while physical activity can partially counteract the effects of a pro-inflammatory diet, it's not a complete solution. It's like trying to fix a leaky roof with an umbrella;

it helps, but it's not a permanent fix. The review highlights that systemic inflammation, adiposity, insulin resistance, and vascular dysfunction are key pathways linking dietary inflammatory exposure to disease vulnerability.

Physical activity can influence these pathways, but its effects vary based on factors like exercise dose, intensity, and individual health status.

It's important to remember that physical activity doesn't negate the dietary exposure, nor should it be seen as a free pass to maintain an unhealthy diet.

Instead, it can act as a modifier, shaping how dietary inflammatory potential translates into disease risk.

This insight is crucial for individuals with prediabetes, as it suggests that a combination of a healthy diet and regular physical activity is more effective in managing inflammation-related health risks than either approach alone.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding how diet and exercise interact to influence inflammation is crucial for individuals with prediabetes, as it can help them make informed decisions about lifestyle changes to manage their health risks.

Practical Takeaways

- Physical activity can partially counteract the effects of a pro-inflammatory diet but is not a complete solution.
- Systemic inflammation, adiposity, insulin resistance, and vascular dysfunction are key pathways linking diet to disease vulnerability.
- The effects of physical activity on inflammation-related health outcomes vary based on exercise dose, intensity, and individual health status.
- Physical activity does not negate dietary exposure and should not be seen as a free pass to maintain an unhealthy diet.
- A combination of a healthy diet and regular physical activity is more effective in managing inflammation-related health risks than either approach alone.
