## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How do different hypocaloric diets with varying macronutrient and protein distribution affect glycemic control and body composition in shift workers with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 126 shift workers with overweight or obesity and prediabetes or type 2 diabetes.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Three-arm, parallel-group, single-blind randomized controlled trial with three hypocaloric diets: (A) high-protein diet with protein-enriched dinner, (B) high-protein diet with protein-restricted dinner, (C) normoproteic control diet.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of glycemic control, body composition, lipid profile, sleep quality, and quality of life between the three diets.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Primary outcomes are changes in glycemic control and DXA-derived body composition. Secondary outcomes include lipid profile, sleep quality, and quality of life.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** The study aims to provide new evidence on how evening protein intake may influence metabolic health in populations exposed to circadian disruption.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is specific to shift workers and may not be generalizable to other populations. The findings are preliminary and await full data analysis.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** Readers with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes who are shift workers may discuss the potential benefits of different dietary protein distributions with their healthcare providers, but should await the study's results for concrete recommendations.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine working the night shift and struggling with high blood sugar. A new study offers hope by looking at how meal timing and protein intake can help manage these issues.

Shift work disrupts our body's natural rhythms, which can lead to poor diet choices and metabolic problems like type 2 diabetes.

This trial focuses on three different diets to see how they affect blood sugar control and body composition in shift workers with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes.

One diet emphasizes a high-protein dinner, another limits evening protein, and the third is a standard protein distribution. The study's findings could guide healthier eating habits for those working against the clock.

It's important to note that while this research is promising, it's not a one-size-fits-all solution. Each person's response to diet can vary, and what works for one might not work for another.

Always consult with a healthcare provider before making significant dietary changes.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Shift work and irregular meal patterns are common issues that can lead to metabolic disorders. Understanding how diet timing and composition can mitigate these risks is crucial for the health of millions of shift workers.

Practical Takeaways

- Chrononutrition, aligning diet with body rhythms, may improve metabolic health in shift workers.
- High-protein diets, especially with a focus on dinner, could be beneficial for blood sugar control.
- Limiting evening protein intake is another dietary approach worth exploring.
- Consult a healthcare provider before making significant changes to your diet based on this research.
