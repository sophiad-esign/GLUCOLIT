## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the impact of physical activity interventions on managing obesity in adolescents aged 10 to 19 years?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Adolescents aged 10 to 19 years with obesity.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Physical activity interventions lasting at least 12 weeks, of any frequency, type, or intensity.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Standard care, waiting list, no physical activity, or other active interventions.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Body weight, BMI, BMI z-score, waist circumference, body fat percentage, glucose, and insulin resistance.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Low-certainty evidence suggests slight improvements in body weight, waist circumference, and body fat percentage with physical activity. Very low-certainty evidence for other outcomes.
- **局限性 / Limits:** High risk of bias in included studies, serious methodological limitations, clinical and statistical heterogeneity, and small-study effects.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While physical activity may have some benefits in managing obesity in adolescents, the current evidence is not strong enough to make definitive recommendations. More high-quality research is needed.

## English Plain-Language Version

Feeling sluggish after a heavy meal? It's like your body is trying to tell you something. Just as a car needs the right fuel to run efficiently, our bodies need the right balance of diet and exercise.

For teenagers living with prediabetes or early metabolic risks, finding the right exercise routine is crucial. A recent systematic review looked into how physical activity can help manage obesity in this age group.

The review found that any form of physical activity might slightly improve body weight, waist circumference, and body fat percentage in adolescents with obesity.

However, the certainty of this evidence is low, and more high-quality studies are needed. It's important to remember that while exercise is beneficial, it's not a standalone solution.

It works best when combined with a healthy diet and other lifestyle changes. If you're thinking about starting an exercise program, it's a good idea to talk to a healthcare provider first.

They can help you find the right balance of frequency, intensity, and types of exercise that are safe and effective for you.

In the meantime, here are some practical steps you can consider: Start with moderate activities like walking or cycling, and gradually increase the intensity.

Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise per week, spread across several days. Listen to your body and rest when needed to avoid injuries.

And remember, consistency is key—try to make exercise a regular part of your routine.

The bottom line is that while we can't turn every association into causation, the evidence does suggest that physical activity plays a role in managing obesity.

It's not about finding the perfect exercise plan, but finding one that works for you and sticking with it.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Obesity in adolescents is a growing concern due to its link with various health issues, including prediabetes and insulin resistance.

Understanding how physical activity can help manage obesity is crucial for developing effective interventions.

Practical Takeaways

- Physical activity may slightly improve body weight, waist circumference, and body fat percentage in adolescents with obesity.
- The certainty of the evidence is low, indicating a need for more high-quality studies.
- Exercise should be combined with a healthy diet and other lifestyle changes for better outcomes.
- Start with moderate activities and gradually increase intensity to avoid injuries.
- Consistency is key in making exercise a regular part of your routine.
