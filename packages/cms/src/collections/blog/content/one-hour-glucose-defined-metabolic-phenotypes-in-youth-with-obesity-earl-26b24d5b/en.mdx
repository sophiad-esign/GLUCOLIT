## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How do 1-hour glucose levels during an oral glucose tolerance test relate to insulin sensitivity and β-cell function in obese youth?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 726 children and adolescents with obesity who underwent an oral glucose tolerance test at a tertiary center.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Classification based on 1-hour plasma glucose levels after an oral glucose tolerance test.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of insulin sensitivity and β-cell function indices between different glucose level groups.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Insulin sensitivity (Matsuda index), insulin exposure (insulin AUC), insulinogenic index at 30 min (IGI30), insulinogenic index at 60 min (IGI60), and Insulin Secretion-Sensitivity Index-2 (ISSI-2).
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** NGT-High group showed lower insulin sensitivity and greater insulin exposure than NGT-Low. Lower IGI30, IGI60, and ISSI-2 indicated early β-cell dysfunction. Delayed insulin peak timing was observed in IGT and advanced dysglycemia groups.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is retrospective and includes a specific population of obese children and adolescents at a tertiary center, which may limit generalizability.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** A high 1-hour glucose level may indicate reduced insulin sensitivity and early β-cell dysfunction in obese youth, suggesting the importance of this measure in metabolic evaluation and potential early intervention.

## English Plain-Language Version

Obese youth with higher 1-hour glucose levels during an oral glucose tolerance test show signs of reduced insulin sensitivity and early β-cell dysfunction.

This suggests that even if fasting glucose is normal, a high 1-hour glucose level could indicate potential metabolic issues.

Understanding these signs can guide lifestyle changes to improve insulin sensitivity and prevent dysglycemia progression.

Fasting plasma glucose, HbA1c, and ISSI-2 are all associated with dysglycemia, providing multiple ways to assess risk. While a high 1-hour glucose level is a risk factor, it does not guarantee diabetes development.

It's essential to discuss these findings with healthcare providers to make informed health decisions.

Lifestyle modifications, including diet and exercise, are crucial in managing risk factors associated with high 1-hour glucose levels.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the early signs of insulin resistance and β-cell dysfunction is crucial for individuals with obesity, as it can help in making lifestyle changes to prevent the progression to more severe forms of dysglycemia and diabetes.

Practical Takeaways

- High 1-hour glucose levels after an oral glucose tolerance test can indicate reduced insulin sensitivity and early β-cell dysfunction, even in those with normal fasting glucose.
- Understanding your 1-hour glucose level can help guide lifestyle changes to improve insulin sensitivity and prevent metabolic issues.
- Multiple markers, including fasting plasma glucose, HbA1c, and ISSI-2, can provide insights into your risk for dysglycemia.
- It's important to discuss these findings with your healthcare provider to understand what they mean for your personal health.
- Lifestyle modifications, including diet and exercise, can be crucial in managing risk factors associated with high 1-hour glucose levels.
