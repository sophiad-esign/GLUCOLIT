## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the association of post-oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) hypoglycemia with clinical features, incident diabetes, and mortality?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 2924 adults without known diabetes, median age 49 years, 1680 women.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Participants underwent a 75-g OGTT and were grouped according to serum glucose at 120 minutes.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparisons were made between groups based on serum glucose levels at 120 minutes post-OGTT.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Incidence of diabetes (median follow-up, 7.5 years) and mortality (median follow-up, 14.5 years).
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Participants with post-OGTT hypoglycemia were younger, leaner, less insulin resistant, and more frequently active smokers. They had the lowest incidence of diabetes and mortality, although these associations were attenuated after adjusting for confounders.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study's findings may be influenced by confounding factors such as age, sex, body mass index, and physical activity. The study population was also limited to Spanish adults, which may affect the generalizability of the results.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes, the findings suggest that maintaining a favorable metabolic profile, including good insulin sensitivity, may be protective against diabetes and mortality. However, it's important to note that post-OGTT hypoglycemia is not a definitive indicator and should be considered within the context of overall health and lifestyle factors.

## English Plain-Language Version

Feeling lightheaded after a glucose test might indicate post-OGTT hypoglycemia, a condition where blood sugar levels drop.

A study investigated this phenomenon, focusing on its association with clinical features, diabetes incidence, and mortality rates.

The research included 2924 adults without known diabetes, who underwent a 75-g OGTT.

Participants with post-OGTT hypoglycemia were found to be younger, leaner, and less insulin resistant, often being active smokers.

Despite their lowest incidence of diabetes and mortality, these associations were less significant after adjusting for confounders.

The findings suggest that post-OGTT hypoglycemia might be a marker of protection against diabetes and mortality, possibly due to preserved early insulin secretion and good insulin sensitivity.

For prediabetes management, maintaining a balanced diet, regular physical activity, and avoiding smoking are crucial to manage insulin sensitivity and prevent diabetes.

It's important to consult with healthcare providers for personalized guidance, as diet and lifestyle changes are not a substitute for medical advice.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding post-OGTT hypoglycemia is crucial for individuals with prediabetes as it can signal underlying metabolic changes affecting their risk of developing diabetes and related health outcomes, guiding lifestyle modifications to

improve insulin sensitivity and overall health.

Practical Takeaways

- Post-OGTT hypoglycemia is linked to younger, leaner individuals with less insulin resistance.
- Balanced diet and regular physical activity are key in managing insulin sensitivity.
- Smoking's association with post-OGTT hypoglycemia adds to the reasons to quit.
- The protective effect of post-OGTT hypoglycemia against diabetes and mortality is not absolute and lessens with confounder adjustments.
- Consult healthcare providers for personalized advice and to address specific health concerns.
