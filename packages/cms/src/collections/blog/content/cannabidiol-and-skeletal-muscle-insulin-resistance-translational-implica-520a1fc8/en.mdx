## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the relationship between cannabidiol (CBD) and insulin resistance, particularly in skeletal muscle, and how does this relate to exercise and rehabilitation medicine?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Individuals with insulin resistance, prediabetes, or early metabolic risk.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Cannabidiol (CBD) as a potential modulator of skeletal muscle insulin resistance.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Preclinical and clinical studies on CBD's effects compared to standard exercise and rehabilitation practices.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Impact on insulin resistance, glycemic control, muscle function, exercise performance, and rehabilitation outcomes.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Preclinical data suggest CBD may influence pathways involved in skeletal muscle insulin resistance. However, human studies are limited and do not consistently show improvements in glycemic control or insulin sensitivity with CBD alone.
- **局限性 / Limits:** Current human studies on CBD are limited and do not demonstrate consistent improvements in glycemic control or insulin sensitivity. Evidence regarding effects on muscle function, exercise performance, or rehabilitation outcomes is lacking.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While CBD shows potential in preclinical studies, it is not a proven treatment for insulin resistance. Exercise remains a key intervention for managing insulin resistance and should be the focus for individuals with prediabetes or early metabolic risk.

## English Plain-Language Version

Feeling sluggish after a long day at work, you might think about a quick workout to boost your energy. Exercise isn't just about immediate energy;

it's a powerful tool against insulin resistance, a condition that can lead to type 2 diabetes. Insulin resistance is like a traffic jam in your body's energy supply lines—glucose builds up because cells can't take it in efficiently.

Regular exercise can help clear these traffic jams, improving how your muscles use glucose.

A recent review looked into cannabidiol (CBD), a compound from cannabis, and its potential to help with insulin resistance, particularly in the muscles. While CBD shows promise in lab studies, it's not a proven treatment yet.

However, the review highlights that exercise is a key part of managing insulin resistance. It's not about finding a magic bullet but building a lifestyle that includes regular physical activity.

Exercise frequency is crucial. Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week, like brisk walking or cycling. Intensity matters too—raise your heart rate but keep it at a level where you can still talk.

Progression is about gradually increasing the duration and intensity to avoid injury and keep challenging your body. Safety is paramount; always warm up and cool down, and listen to your body to avoid overexertion.

Adherence is about making exercise a habit, finding activities you enjoy, and setting realistic goals.

While CBD's role is still being explored, the evidence is clear on exercise. It's not just about burning calories; it's about improving your body's ability to use energy efficiently.

Incorporating regular exercise into your routine can help manage insulin resistance and reduce the risk of type 2 diabetes.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Insulin resistance is a precursor to type 2 diabetes and is associated with metabolic syndrome, affecting a significant portion of the population.

Exercise is a non-pharmacological intervention that can help improve insulin sensitivity and is accessible to most people.

Practical Takeaways

- Regular exercise is a key strategy in managing insulin resistance.
- Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week.
- Intensity and progression in exercise are important for safety and effectiveness.
- Adherence to an exercise routine is crucial for long-term health benefits.
- CBD is still experimental; focus on proven lifestyle changes like regular physical activity.
