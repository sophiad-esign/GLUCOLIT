## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How do heat shock proteins, particularly HSP70, modulate insulin resistance and what is the potential of exercise-induced HSP70 expression as a therapeutic target for improving insulin sensitivity and metabolic health?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Individuals with insulin resistance or early metabolic risk
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Exercise and heat shock protein (HSP) activation
- **对照 / Comparison:** The review compares the effects of exercise on HSP70 expression and insulin resistance
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Improved insulin sensitivity, reduced risk of metabolic disorders, activation of heat shock response, and enhancement of HSP70 expression
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Exercise can activate HSP70, which may improve insulin sensitivity and protect against metabolic dysfunction. The relationship between HSP70 and insulin resistance is complex and not fully understood.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The exact mechanisms by which exercise improves insulin resistance are still being studied, and individual responses to exercise can vary.

## 原文精华摘要

想象一下，一天早晨醒来，你发现车子因为电池没电而无法启动。 就像汽车需要充满电的电池才能工作一样，我们的身体需要能够对胰岛素做出反应的细胞来维持代谢健康。 胰岛素抵抗就像我们细胞中的一个没电的电池，阻止它们吸收葡萄糖，这可能导致2型糖尿病。

运动就像给电池充电——它可以激活热休克蛋白（HSPs），特别是HSP70，帮助细胞更好地响应胰岛素。 本文综述了HSP70如何在我们细胞内工作，以及运动如何提高其水平，可能改善胰岛素敏感性和减少代谢紊乱的风险。

热休克蛋白就像身体的维修队，帮助维持细胞秩序和对抗压力的弹性。 当发生胰岛素抵抗时，通常是由于氧化应激和炎症增加，HSPs可以帮助管理这些问题。 众所周知，运动可以改善胰岛素抵抗，这篇综述表明，它通过激活HSP70来实现这一点，增强了保护细胞免受代谢功能障碍的细胞适应性。

然而，HSP70与胰岛素抵抗之间的关系复杂，尚未完全理解。 综述讨论了HSP70调节胰岛素抵抗、线粒体功能和骨骼肌炎症反应的途径。 它还探讨了运动诱导的HSP70表达作为改善胰岛素敏感性的潜在治疗靶点。

虽然确切的机制仍在研究中，但很明显运动对胰岛素抵抗有积极影响。 需要注意的是，这不是一个放之四海而皆准的解决方案，个体对运动的反应可能会有所不同。 综述表明，运动可以激活HSP70，这可能有助于细胞更好地处理葡萄糖，但需要更多的研究来完全理解这种关系。

与此同时，将定期运动纳入你的日常生活可以是迈向更好代谢健康的积极一步。

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine waking up one morning and realizing your car won't start because the battery is dead. Just like a car needs a charged battery to function, our bodies need cells that can respond to insulin to maintain metabolic health.

Insulin resistance is like a dead battery in our cells, preventing them from taking in glucose, which can lead to type 2 diabetes.

Exercise is like jump-starting the battery—it can activate heat shock proteins (HSPs), particularly HSP70, which help cells respond better to insulin.

This article reviews how HSP70 works within our cells and how exercise can boost its levels, potentially improving insulin sensitivity and reducing the risk of metabolic disorders.

Heat shock proteins are like the body's repair crew, helping to maintain cellular order and resilience against stress.

When insulin resistance occurs, it's often due to increased oxidative stress and inflammation, which HSPs can help manage.

Exercise is known to improve insulin resistance, and this review suggests that it does so by activating HSP70, which enhances cellular adaptations that protect against metabolic dysfunction.

However, the relationship between HSP70 and insulin resistance is complex and not yet fully understood.

The review discusses the pathways by which HSP70 can modulate insulin resistance, mitochondrial function, and inflammatory responses in skeletal muscle.

It also explores the potential of exercise-induced HSP70 expression as a therapeutic target for improving insulin sensitivity.

While the exact mechanisms are still being studied, it's clear that exercise can have a positive impact on insulin resistance.

It's important to note that this is not a one-size-fits-all solution, and individual responses to exercise can vary.

The review suggests that exercise can activate HSP70, which may help cells better handle glucose, but more research is needed to fully understand this relationship.

In the meantime, incorporating regular exercise into your routine can be a proactive step towards better metabolic health.

GLUCOLIT insight: As we learn more about the role of HSP70 in insulin resistance, there may be opportunities to develop targeted interventions that enhance the body's natural stress response.

This could lead to new approaches for improving insulin sensitivity and preventing type 2 diabetes. In the meantime, focusing on regular exercise and a healthy lifestyle can help activate our body's own protective mechanisms.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Insulin resistance is a precursor to type 2 diabetes and other metabolic disorders.

Understanding how exercise can activate heat shock proteins to improve insulin sensitivity is crucial for developing lifestyle modifications that can prevent or delay the onset of these conditions.

Practical Takeaways

- The relationship between HSP70 and insulin resistance is complex and not yet fully understood.
- Incorporating regular exercise into your routine can be a proactive step towards better metabolic health.
