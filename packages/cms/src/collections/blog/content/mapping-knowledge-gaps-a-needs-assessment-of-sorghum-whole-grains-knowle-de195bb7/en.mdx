## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the knowledge level of university students regarding whole grains, sorghum, and prediabetes risk?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Undergraduate and graduate students aged 18-44 years.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Survey on knowledge and practices related to whole grains, sorghum, and prediabetes risk.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of prediabetes risk and knowledge levels between male and female students.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Prediabetes risk based on the CDC Prediabetes Risk Test, knowledge scores on whole grains and sorghum, and physical activity levels.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** 63% of students were at risk of prediabetes. Males had a significantly higher risk of prediabetes and T2DM compared to females. Students scored low on overall knowledge about whole grains and sorghum. Despite high physical activity levels, many students were overweight or obese.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was conducted on a specific population of university students and may not be generalizable to all individuals with prediabetes.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** This study suggests that increasing knowledge about whole grains and sorghum, and their role in managing prediabetes risk, could be beneficial for individuals, especially those who are overweight or obese.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're a student, busy with classes and assignments, and you're trying to eat healthily but aren't sure what's best for your blood sugar levels.

A recent study surveyed university students about their knowledge of whole grains, sorghum, and prediabetes risk. It found that many students, especially males, are at risk of prediabetes and have low knowledge about these topics.

Despite reporting high physical activity levels, many students were overweight or obese. This highlights the importance of not just exercising but also eating a balanced diet, rich in whole grains and sorghum, to manage prediabetes risk.

The study showed that students with obesity had significantly higher odds of prediabetes compared to those with normal weight. This suggests that weight management is crucial in prediabetes prevention.

Incorporating more whole grains and sorghum into your diet can help with weight control and improve blood sugar levels. It's not just about the quantity of food but also the quality.

Social media was the primary source of nutrition information for many students, which might not always provide accurate or comprehensive advice. It's important to seek reliable sources for dietary guidance.

For those with prediabetes, a diet rich in whole grains and sorghum, combined with regular physical activity, can be a practical approach to managing risk.

Remember, this is not medical advice, but rather a reflection on the study's findings. Always consult with a healthcare provider for personalized guidance.

The study's results underscore the need for more education on the benefits of whole grains and sorghum in managing prediabetes.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

This study is relevant because it addresses a gap in knowledge among university students about the importance of whole grains and sorghum in managing prediabetes risk.

It provides insights into the lifestyle factors that contribute to prediabetes and suggests practical dietary changes that can be made to reduce this risk.
