## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How does fructose-induced prediabetes affect DNA methylation in white adipose tissue, and do these changes persist after metabolic normalization?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Male rats were fed a standard diet and then divided into three groups: Control (tap water), Fructose (10% fructose solution), and FC (10% fructose solution for 21 days followed by tap water for 49 days).
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Fructose consumption leading to a prediabetic state in rats.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of DNA methylation in visceral white adipose tissue between the control group and the fructose-exposed groups before and after dietary normalization.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Identification of 1151 differentially methylated CpG sites and 330 genes enriched in oxidative phosphorylation and thermogenesis pathways in the visceral white adipose tissue of fructose-exposed rats.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Fructose intake induced a prediabetic phenotype, which was reversed by dietary normalization. Despite metabolic recovery, several methylation alterations persisted, indicating incomplete epigenetic reversal.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was conducted in rats, and while the findings suggest potential implications for humans, direct extrapolation is limited. The study does not establish causation but shows an association between fructose consumption and DNA methylation changes.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** Readers should understand that a high-fructose diet may lead to persistent epigenetic changes in white adipose tissue, potentially affecting metabolism and the risk of developing type 2 diabetes. However, the study's findings should not be overclaimed, and the association does not imply causation. It is important to consider the long-term effects of dietary choices on health and to discuss any significant dietary changes with a healthcare provider.

A study in Molecular and Cellular Endocrinology shows that a high-fructose diet can lead to lasting changes in fat cell DNA, even after blood sugar levels normalize. These changes might be linked to the development of type 2 diabetes.

The study involved rats, suggesting potential implications for human health. Understanding how diet affects genes can guide better food choices to prevent prediabetes and metabolic disorders.

White adipose tissue, crucial for glucose and lipid regulation, was studied. Rats on a high-fructose diet developed a prediabetic state, with some DNA methylation changes persisting after returning to a normal diet.

DNA methylation can turn genes on or off, affecting cellular function. Specific genes related to oxidative phosphorylation and thermogenesis were affected by fructose, key for fat cell metabolism regulation.

While the study shows an association between fructose and DNA methylation, it doesn't prove causation. More research is needed to understand the full implications.

However, it suggests that a high-fructose diet might have long-term health effects, even after consumption stops. This underscores the importance of today's food choices for future health.

For those concerned about prediabetes, the study emphasizes the importance of a balanced diet. It's about making choices that support healthy metabolism, like choosing whole fruits over high-fructose fruit juices or reducing sugar intake.

Discussing dietary changes with a healthcare provider ensures they are safe and appropriate for individual health needs.

In conclusion, the link between fructose and DNA methylation is complex, but the study provides insights into how diet can influence health at a genetic level.

Informed food choices can help reduce the risk of prediabetes and other metabolic disorders.
