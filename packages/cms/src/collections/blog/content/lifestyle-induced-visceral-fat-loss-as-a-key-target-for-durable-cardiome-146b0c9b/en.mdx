## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Do lifestyle interventions that reduce abdominal fat have long-term benefits for cardiometabolic health despite weight regain?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Participants from two randomized controlled trials (CENTRAL and DIRECT-PLUS) with abdominal obesity or dyslipidemia.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Dietary patterns (low-fat, Mediterranean diet variants) combined with structured physical activity.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Long-term follow-up (5 and 10 years) after the intervention, comparing changes in abdominal fat depots and cardiometabolic profiles.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Partial preservation of abdominal fat reduction and improvements in Metabolic Score for Insulin Resistance, composite risk score, and Metabolic Syndrome Severity Score.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Despite complete weight regain, waist circumference and abdominal fat depots partially preserved their intervention-induced achievements at long-term follow-up. A 10% reduction in visceral fat was associated with a 28% lower risk of incident type 2 diabetes.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study relied on self-reported diet adherence and physical activity scores at follow-up, which may introduce bias.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes or insulin resistance, focusing on reducing belly fat through diet and exercise may lead to long-term improvements in cardiometabolic health, even if weight is regained.

## English Plain-Language Version

At a family gathering, surrounded by delicious food, one might wonder how to enjoy the meal without jeopardizing health, especially with prediabetes.

A study suggests that reducing belly fat, not just weight, could be key to managing such conditions.

Even if weight is regained, losing belly fat through diet and exercise can lead to long-term improvements in heart and metabolic health, reducing the risk of type 2 diabetes by nearly 30%.

The study followed participants from two clinical trials for 5 to 10 years. Despite regaining weight, many maintained some reduction in belly fat, linked to better insulin sensitivity and lower diabetes risk.

This indicates that diet and exercise effects are about more than just weight loss—they also involve where the fat is stored. Targeting belly fat can lead to sustainable health changes.

It's important to note that this doesn't mean one can eat whatever they want as long as they exercise. The study emphasized a healthy diet, including low-fat and Mediterranean-style diets, and regular physical activity.

A 10% reduction in belly fat can significantly improve health outcomes. Planning meals and activities with belly fat reduction in mind can lead to substantial health benefits.

Remember, this is a long-term strategy requiring consistent, healthy choices. Always consult with a healthcare provider before making significant changes to diet or exercise routines.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

This study is significant as it provides long-term evidence that lifestyle changes, particularly those targeting belly fat, can offer lasting benefits for individuals with prediabetes or insulin resistance, beyond just weight loss.

Practical Takeaways

- Reducing belly fat is crucial for long-term metabolic health.
- Reducing belly fat can lead to health improvements, even with weight regain.
- A 10% reduction in belly fat can lower the risk of type 2 diabetes by nearly 30%.
- Emphasize low-fat, Mediterranean-style diets and regular physical activity.
- Consult healthcare providers before making significant lifestyle changes.
