## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Does chronic activation of mTORC1 in skeletal muscle directly cause glucose intolerance?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Six-month-old female and male mice with tamoxifen-inducible, muscle-specific knockout of Depdc5
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Mice were fed normal chow or western diet (45% fat, 17% sucrose) for 12 weeks
- **对照 / Comparison:** Depdc5 knockout (KO) mice vs. control mice under different dietary conditions
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Glucose homeostasis, body and fat mass, glucose tolerance, inflammatory markers
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** KO increased mTORC1 signaling and altered autophagy markers. Western diet increased body and fat mass and impaired glucose tolerance independent of genotype. KO had minimal effects on fasting glucose, insulin, HOMA-IR, HbA1c, or oral glucose tolerance.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was conducted in mice, and results may not directly translate to humans. The study's design does not allow for conclusions about causation between mTORC1 activation and glucose intolerance in humans.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While the study in mice suggests that chronic mTORC1 activation in skeletal muscle promotes inflammatory remodeling, it is not sufficient to impair systemic glucose homeostasis under normal dietary conditions or even under dietary stress without a sedentary lifestyle. This highlights the importance of regular physical activity in managing prediabetes.

## English Plain-Language Version

Feeling sluggish after a long day at work, you decide to skip the gym again. But what if that workout could be the key to managing your prediabetes? Exercise is not just about physical fitness;

it's a powerful tool in the fight against insulin resistance and glucose intolerance.

A recent study sheds light on how our bodies respond to different diets and exercise regimens, offering insights into how we can take control of our health.

The study involved mice with altered mTORC1 signaling in their skeletal muscles, which is linked to aging and insulin resistance.

While the study's findings are complex, the takeaway is clear: regular exercise and a balanced diet are crucial for maintaining glucose homeostasis.

Even under the stress of a high-fat, high-sugar diet, the mice with increased mTORC1 signaling didn't show significant glucose intolerance unless they were also sedentary.

This suggests that while diet is important, exercise plays a pivotal role in managing prediabetes. It's not just about avoiding weight gain; exercise can also help regulate inflammation and immune responses in our bodies.

So, how can we apply this to our daily lives? Incorporating regular physical activity into your routine is essential.

Whether it's a brisk walk after dinner or a weekly sports game with friends, finding an activity you enjoy can make all the difference. And remember, consistency is key—aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week.

As for diet, balance is the name of the game. Opt for whole foods, limit processed items, and don't shy away from discussing your dietary needs with a healthcare provider. They can help you craft a meal plan that supports your health goals.

The study's findings remind us that managing prediabetes is a multifaceted approach, and while we can't change our genetics, we can take steps to support our health through exercise and diet.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Exercise and diet are two pillars of lifestyle management for individuals with prediabetes, insulin resistance, or early metabolic risk.

Understanding how these factors interact with our bodies can empower us to make informed decisions about our health.

Practical Takeaways

- Regular exercise is crucial for managing prediabetes and insulin resistance.
- A balanced diet, rich in whole foods, can support glucose homeostasis.
- Incorporating physical activity into your routine can help regulate inflammation and immune responses.
- Consistency in exercise is key—aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week.
- Discuss your dietary and exercise needs with a healthcare provider to craft a personalized plan.
