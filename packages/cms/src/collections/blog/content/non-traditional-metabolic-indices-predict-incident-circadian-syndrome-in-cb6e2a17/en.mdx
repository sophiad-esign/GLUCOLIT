## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Can composite metabolic indices predict the development of circadian syndrome, which includes disturbed sleep, in middle-aged and older Chinese adults?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 4,325 Chinese adults aged 45 years or older, free of circadian syndrome at baseline.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Analysis of eight baseline metabolic composites.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of metabolic indices and their association with incident circadian syndrome over a four-year period.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** 1,025 incident cases of circadian syndrome (23.7%).
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Every index remained independently linked to circadian syndrome after multivariable adjustment. The triglyceride-glucose body mass index (TyG-BMI) showed the steepest positive gradient, while the estimated glucose disposal rate (eGDR) demonstrated the most pronounced inverse gradient.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study's findings are specific to a Chinese population and may not be generalizable to other ethnicities. The study design does not establish causation, only association.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes, understanding the relationship between metabolic health and sleep is important. Monitoring certain metabolic markers could help identify those at risk of developing circadian syndrome, emphasizing the need for proactive sleep management to improve metabolic outcomes.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine waking up refreshed every morning, ready to tackle the day. Now, imagine that feeling is linked to how well your body handles sugar. That's the power of sleep, especially for those with prediabetes.

A recent study in China found that certain metabolic markers can predict circadian syndrome, which includes poor sleep. This is important because managing sleep could help manage your metabolic health.

The study looked at 4,325 adults over four years and found that those with higher triglyceride-glucose body mass index (TyG-BMI) were more likely to develop circadian syndrome.

On the other hand, a higher estimated glucose disposal rate (eGDR) was linked to a lower risk.

These findings suggest that simple tests could help identify those at risk of circadian syndrome, allowing for earlier interventions to improve sleep and metabolic health. It's not just about getting more sleep;

it's about the quality and timing of sleep that matters. Incorporating these insights into your routine could be a step towards better health.

Remember, while this study provides valuable information, it's always best to discuss any changes with your healthcare provider.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Sleep is often overlooked in discussions about metabolic health, but it plays a critical role in how our bodies process glucose and manage insulin resistance.

This study highlights the importance of sleep in the context of prediabetes and metabolic syndrome, showing that certain metabolic markers can predict the development of circadian syndrome, which includes sleep disturbances.

Understanding these links can help individuals with prediabetes take proactive steps to improve their sleep habits, potentially reducing their risk of developing full-blown diabetes and other metabolic disorders.
