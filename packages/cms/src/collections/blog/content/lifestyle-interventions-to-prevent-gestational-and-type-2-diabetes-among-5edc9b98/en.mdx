## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What are the effects of culturally adapted lifestyle interventions on preventing gestational and type 2 diabetes among migrant women from low- and middle-income countries?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Migrant women from low- and middle-income countries living in high-income settings.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Culturally adapted lifestyle interventions focusing on diet, exercise, and cultural engagement.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparisons made between culturally adapted interventions and standard care or other interventions.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Behavioral and metabolic outcomes related to diabetes prevention, including dietary behaviors, physical activity, glycaemic measures, and diabetes-related knowledge.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Culturally adapted, multi-component lifestyle interventions show modest but inconsistent improvements in behavioral and metabolic outcomes among migrant women from low- and middle-income countries with overall low-to-moderate certainty of evidence.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The evidence base is limited, with studies varying in quality and heterogeneity, making it difficult to draw definitive conclusions.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For readers with prediabetes or insulin resistance, culturally adapted lifestyle interventions may offer a more effective approach to diabetes prevention, but the evidence suggests that more high-quality, tailored research is needed.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're a migrant woman trying to navigate a new culture and a new diet. The changes can be overwhelming, especially when you're at risk of diabetes.

A recent review looked at how lifestyle changes can help women like you manage this risk. The studies showed that programs that adapt to your culture, like using your language or teaching about foods you know, can make a difference.

They found that not only did these programs help with diet and exercise, but they also improved blood sugar levels. It's not a one-size-fits-all approach; it's about meeting you where you are.

The review also pointed out that these programs need to be more than just about exercise—they work best when they include changes in diet and are tailored to your culture. It's about finding a balance that fits your life.

And while the results were promising, they also showed that there's still a lot we don't know. The studies were different in many ways, making it hard to say exactly what works best.

But one thing is clear: there's a need for more programs that respect your culture and your background.

So, what does this mean for you? It means that when you're looking at ways to prevent diabetes, it's worth asking about programs that take your culture into account.

It's about finding a program that speaks your language, literally and figuratively. And remember, it's not just about what you eat or how much you exercise, but also about how these changes fit into your life.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Diabetes is a growing concern for many, especially for migrant women who face additional challenges due to cultural and lifestyle changes.

Understanding how culturally adapted interventions can help manage this risk is crucial for developing effective prevention strategies.

Practical Takeaways

- Culturally tailored lifestyle interventions can help prevent diabetes in migrant women.
- These interventions work best when they include diet changes and are adapted to the culture.
- Programs that use your language and teach about familiar foods can make a difference.
- There's a need for more programs that respect the cultural background of migrant women.
- Finding a balance that fits your life is key to preventing diabetes.
