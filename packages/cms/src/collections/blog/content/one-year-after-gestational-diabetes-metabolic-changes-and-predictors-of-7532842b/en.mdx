## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How do glycaemic and metabolic changes occur one year after delivery in women with previous gestational diabetes, and what are the clinical and lifestyle predictors of postpartum glucose impairment?
- **研究对象 / Population:** A cohort of 134 women with prior gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) assessed at 6-12 weeks and one year postpartum.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Assessment of glycaemic and metabolic changes, dietary habits using a 3-day food diary and the PREDIMED questionnaire, and physical activity using the International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ).
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of metabolic parameters and glucose tolerance at baseline and one year postpartum.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Increased prevalence of altered OGTT and T2DM, identification of insulin therapy during pregnancy as a predictor of dysglycaemia at one year postpartum.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** At baseline, 32.9% of women showed altered OGTT; this increased to 38.8% at one year, while T2DM prevalence rose from 2.2 to 5.2%. Insulin therapy during pregnancy was the only independent predictor of dysglycaemia at one year postpartum.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was conducted on a relatively small cohort of women with prior GDM and may not be representative of all women with GDM.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** Women with a history of GDM should be vigilant about their diet and glucose levels postpartum, as metabolic changes can occur within the first year, increasing the risk of developing T2DM. Lifestyle interventions, particularly dietary modifications, are crucial in managing this risk.

## English Plain-Language Version

After giving birth, women who had gestational diabetes face a higher risk of developing type 2 diabetes. A recent study followed 134 women with previous GDM for a year, finding that metabolic changes and glucose levels worsened over time.

Here's what you can do: keep a food diary to track your diet, consider swapping high-glycemic foods for healthier options, and discuss a meal plan with your healthcare provider. Remember, every small change counts towards better health.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Women with a history of gestational diabetes are at a significantly higher risk of developing type 2 diabetes, making it crucial to understand and manage postpartum metabolic changes through lifestyle interventions, particularly dietary

modifications.

Practical Takeaways

- Women with a history of gestational diabetes are at a nearly 10-fold higher risk of developing type 2 diabetes.
- Keeping a food diary can help monitor dietary habits and identify areas for improvement.
- Swapping high-glycemic foods like white bread for whole grains can have a positive impact on blood sugar levels.
- Discussing a meal plan with a healthcare provider can provide personalized guidance on managing postpartum glucose levels.
- Every small dietary change contributes to long-term metabolic health and the prevention of type 2 diabetes.
