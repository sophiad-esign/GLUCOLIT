## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What are the barriers and facilitators to detecting and managing pre-diabetes in Singapore's multiethnic population?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Singapore citizens or permanent residents diagnosed with pre-diabetes, aged 21 and above, and conversant in at least one local language.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Lifestyle changes, including diet and exercise habits, after a pre-diabetes diagnosis.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Personal experiences before and after the pre-diabetes diagnosis.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Barriers to lifestyle changes, enablers of pre-diabetes management, and suggestions for improving care.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Personal factors, social and environmental barriers, financial burden, work-related factors, and systemic gaps were identified as barriers. Enablers included personal drivers, family and peer support, healthcare team factors, and knowledge about pre-diabetes.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was qualitative and focused on a specific population in Singapore, which may limit the generalizability of the findings.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with pre-diabetes, understanding the personal and systemic challenges to lifestyle changes can help in developing tailored strategies to manage their condition effectively.

## English Plain-Language Version

Feeling sluggish after a long day at work, you might not think about hitting the gym. But for those with pre-diabetes, regular exercise is more than just a good idea—it's a necessity.

A recent study in Singapore shed light on the challenges faced by individuals with pre-diabetes, particularly when it comes to incorporating exercise into their daily routines.

The study found that personal factors, such as the struggle to sustain behavioral changes, were significant barriers.

However, fear of developing type 2 diabetes and support from family and peers were key motivators for maintaining an active lifestyle.

Understanding these insights can help pre-diabetes patients tailor their exercise routines to fit their needs and overcome obstacles. Exercise doesn't have to be intense; even moderate physical activity can make a difference.

The key is to find a routine that is sustainable and enjoyable, which can be as simple as a daily walk or cycling to work.

Incorporating these activities into your day can help improve your health and reduce the risk of progressing to type 2 diabetes.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Exercise is a critical component of lifestyle management for individuals with pre-diabetes.

Understanding the barriers and motivators for exercise can help patients develop strategies to incorporate physical activity into their daily routines, which is essential for preventing the progression to type 2 diabetes.

Practical Takeaways

- Regular exercise is crucial for managing pre-diabetes.
- Personal motivation and social support are key to sustaining exercise routines.
- Moderate physical activity, like walking or cycling, can be beneficial.
- Finding a sustainable and enjoyable exercise routine is essential.
- Incorporating exercise into daily life can help reduce the risk of type 2 diabetes.
