## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How effective is a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity in individuals with overweight or obesity for weight loss and metabolic improvements?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Adults with a BMI ≥ 30 or ≥ 27 with comorbidities such as pre-diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, obstructive sleep apnea, or cardiovascular disease.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Counseling on a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity, with one group also receiving oral semaglutide.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison between the group receiving semaglutide and the control group.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Weight loss, non-invasive markers of liver fibrosis, and cardiometabolic parameters.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Significant weight loss and improvements in liver parameters and cardiometabolic health in the semaglutide group compared to the control group.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study focused on a specific population with overweight or obesity and did not include individuals with diabetes mellitus.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes and overweight or obesity, a structured approach to diet and lifestyle changes, potentially with medication support, can lead to significant health improvements.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're at a family dinner, and you're trying to manage your prediabetes. The key takeaway? A well-structured diet can significantly improve your health outcomes.

Recent research shows that with a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity, individuals with overweight or obesity can achieve substantial weight loss and metabolic improvements.

This is not just about cutting calories but also about making smart food choices. For example, swapping white rice for brown or adding more vegetables to your plate can make a difference. Portion control is crucial too;

a smaller plate can help you eat less without feeling deprived. And remember, consistency is key—making these changes a part of your routine can lead to long-term benefits.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Prediabetes is a critical stage where lifestyle modifications can prevent or delay the onset of type 2 diabetes. Understanding the impact of diet on glucose control and metabolic health is essential for individuals at this stage.

This article provides practical insights based on recent research, emphasizing the importance of a structured approach to diet and lifestyle changes.

Practical Takeaways

- A structured diet and lifestyle changes are crucial for managing prediabetes.
- Smart food swaps and portion control can lead to significant health improvements.
- Consistency in dietary habits is key for long-term benefits.
- Discuss practical meal plans with your healthcare provider to tailor interventions to your needs.
