## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the association between sleep profiles and glycemic outcomes in adults with prediabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Adults with prediabetes
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Sleep profiles including chronotype, sleep duration, and sleep-wake time
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of glycemic outcomes between different sleep profiles
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Fasting plasma glucose, 2-hour postprandial glucose, glycated hemoglobin, and 24-hour glucose profiles
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Evening chronotypes and shorter sleep durations were associated with adverse glycemic outcomes. Social jet lag was associated with lower post-meal blood sugar levels.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study's findings are associative and do not establish causation. The sample size was relatively small, and the study population may not be representative of all adults with prediabetes.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** Readers with prediabetes should be aware that their sleep patterns may influence their blood sugar levels and consider incorporating good sleep hygiene into their lifestyle to manage their condition.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine waking up feeling refreshed and energized, ready to tackle the day. Now, consider the opposite: waking up groggy and tired, struggling to focus.

Your sleep patterns can significantly impact your health, especially if you have prediabetes. A recent study sheds light on how sleep profiles, including the time you go to bed and wake up, affect your blood sugar levels.

The study found that people who are 'evening types' and have shorter sleep durations tend to have higher blood sugar levels throughout the day.

This is important because managing blood sugar is crucial for those with prediabetes to prevent the progression to type 2 diabetes.

The study also noted that those with a larger discrepancy between their weekday and weekend wake times, known as social jet lag, had lower post-meal blood sugar levels, possibly due to compensatory sleep on weekends.

However, this doesn't mean that staying up late is beneficial; it could be a sign of poor sleep habits that need to be addressed. Incorporating good sleep hygiene into your lifestyle can be a powerful tool in managing prediabetes.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Sleep is a fundamental aspect of our daily lives, and its quality can significantly influence our health, especially for those with prediabetes.

Understanding the connection between sleep patterns and blood sugar levels can help individuals make informed decisions about their sleep habits to better manage their condition.

Practical Takeaways

- Good sleep hygiene is crucial for managing prediabetes.
- Evening types and those with shorter sleep durations may have higher blood sugar levels.
- Social jet lag, or a large discrepancy in wake times between weekdays and weekends, can affect blood sugar levels.
- Incorporating regular sleep patterns into your lifestyle can help improve glycemic outcomes.
