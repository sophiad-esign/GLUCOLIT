## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What are the experiences and preferences of Hispanic men in the National Diabetes Prevention Program?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 17 Hispanic men engaged in the NDPP for at least 4 sessions, majority foreign-born and English proficient.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Participation in the National Diabetes Prevention Program, an intensive lifestyle change intervention.
- **对照 / Comparison:** N/A (qualitative study)
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Experiences and preferences regarding the program, including motivators for enrollment, relief from gaining knowledge, and impressions of the program.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Hispanic men motivated by fear of diabetes, appreciated program resources, and found men-only groups emotionally safe, but had mixed feelings about virtual formats.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is qualitative and based on a small, specific sample of Hispanic men, limiting generalizability.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While the study provides insights into Hispanic men's experiences, it should not be taken as representative of all such individuals' views. The findings suggest areas for potential program improvements.

## English Plain-Language Version

Why it matters Hispanic men are disproportionately affected by diabetes and underrepresented in prevention programs, highlighting the need for understanding their experiences.

What the evidence says Participants appreciated the NDPP's resources and structure, finding them valuable for diabetes prevention knowledge.

What this means Fear of diabetes was a key motivator for enrollment. The program's cultural and gender considerations were appreciated, yet the virtual format received mixed reviews.

What readers can do Program recruitment could be improved by emphasizing the reduction of prediabetes uncertainty and clear communication of the program's structure.

Offering diverse delivery formats may enhance sustained engagement.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the experiences of Hispanic men in diabetes prevention programs is essential for tailoring effective interventions that can reduce health disparities.

Practical Takeaways

- Hispanic men value the knowledge and skills gained from diabetes prevention programs.
- Culturally tailored content and gender-tailored structure are appreciated by participants.
- There is a mixed reception to virtual program formats among Hispanic men.
- Clear communication about the program structure is necessary to improve recruitment.
