## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How does nutrition, sleep quality, and depression levels compare between children with insulin resistance and healthy peers?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 60 children aged 10-18, 30 with insulin resistance and 30 healthy controls.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Assessment of nutritional status, sleep quality, and depression levels.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Children with insulin resistance vs. healthy children.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Significantly higher body weights, BMI, waist circumferences, body fat ratios, and BMI z-scores in the insulin resistance group. Poorer sleep quality and higher depression levels as measured by PSQI and CDI.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Children with insulin resistance had worse nutritional status, sleep quality, and higher depression levels compared to healthy controls.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study's limitations include a relatively small sample size and the cross-sectional nature of the data, which limits causal inference.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While this study does not prove causation, it suggests that children with insulin resistance may benefit from interventions targeting diet, sleep, and mental health to improve their overall condition.

## English Plain-Language Version

Diet, sleep, and mental health are intertwined in children's lives. Those with insulin resistance face higher body weights and fat ratios, along with poorer sleep and higher depression levels.

Addressing these areas is crucial for their health. The study compared 30 children with insulin resistance to 30 healthy peers, using tools like the Mediterranean Diet Quality Index and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index.

The findings highlight a clear need for lifestyle changes. For diet, consider a Mediterranean-style diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and healthy fats. For sleep, aim for consistent, quality rest.

And for mental health, seek support when signs of depression appear. These steps can be discussed with healthcare providers to tailor a plan that fits each child's needs.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Insulin resistance in children is a growing concern with implications for long-term health. Understanding the interplay between nutrition, sleep, and mental health provides a more holistic approach to managing this condition.

Practical Takeaways

- A comprehensive approach to health for children with insulin resistance includes diet, sleep, and mental health.
- A Mediterranean-style diet may offer benefits for managing insulin resistance.
- Consistent, quality sleep is crucial for children's health and can impact insulin resistance.
- Depression is linked to insulin resistance, and addressing mental health is important.
- Lifestyle changes should be tailored to each child's specific needs and discussed with healthcare providers.
