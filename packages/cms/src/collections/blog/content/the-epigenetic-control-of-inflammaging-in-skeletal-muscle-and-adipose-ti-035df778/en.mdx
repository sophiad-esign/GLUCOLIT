## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How does exercise influence the epigenetic regulation of inflammaging in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Individuals with prediabetes, insulin resistance, or early metabolic risk.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Lifestyle interventions, including caloric restriction and physical exercise.
- **对照 / Comparison:** The effects of aging on cellular function and systemic homeostasis, particularly in skeletal muscle and white adipose tissue.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Reversal of age-related effects through epigenetic modifications associated with improved mitochondrial function, enhanced myogenesis, better adipocyte health, and reduced inflammatory gene expression.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Aerobic and resistance exercise promote epigenetic modifications that can improve metabolic health and reduce inflammation.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study provides insights into the mechanisms but does not offer a personalized exercise prescription. The applicability of these findings may vary among individuals.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While this research does not provide a specific exercise regimen, it underscores the importance of regular physical activity in managing metabolic health and suggests that both aerobic and resistance exercises can have positive effects on gene expression related to inflammation and insulin resistance.

## English Plain-Language Version

Feeling sluggish after a long day at work, you might think about hitting the gym to boost your energy. But did you know that exercise could also be a powerful tool to combat the inflammation and insulin resistance that come with age?

It's not just about feeling better; it's about turning back the clock on your cells. As we age, our muscles and fat tissues change, leading to inflammation and a resistance to insulin, which can make us more prone to diseases.

But there's good news: regular exercise can help reverse these effects. Studies show that both aerobic and resistance training can change how our genes are expressed, improving mitochondrial function and reducing inflammation.

This means that with the right kind of movement, we can keep our metabolism young and healthy.

It's not a one-size-fits-all prescription, but understanding how exercise can help us on a cellular level can guide us in making better lifestyle choices.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Exercise is a critical component of managing prediabetes and insulin resistance. Understanding the cellular mechanisms by which exercise impacts health can empower individuals to make informed decisions about their physical activity.

Practical Takeaways

- Regular exercise can help combat age-related inflammation and insulin resistance.
- Both aerobic and resistance exercises are beneficial for metabolic health.
- Exercise influences gene expression, potentially improving mitochondrial function and reducing inflammation.
- Lifestyle changes, including the right kind of exercise, can be a powerful tool for health preservation.
