## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the association between diabetes, pre-diabetes, and cancer incidence among Hispanic/Latino adults?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 16,415 Hispanic/Latino adults from the Hispanic Community Health Study/Study of Latinos (HCHS/SOL).
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Time-varying measures of diabetes status, glycemic control, and insulin resistance.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparisons made between no diabetes, pre-diabetes, controlled diabetes, uncontrolled diabetes, and different levels of insulin resistance.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Incident cancers diagnosed from visit 1 through 2021, including 330 obesity-related cancers.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Pre-diabetes and diabetes were associated with increased hazard ratios for overall cancer and obesity-related cancer risk.
- **局限性 / Limits:** While the study shows associations, it does not establish causation. The study's estimates for obesity-related cancers were less precise.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** The findings suggest that for Hispanic/Latino adults, managing diabetes and pre-diabetes could potentially reduce the risk of developing cancer, emphasizing the importance of diabetes prevention and control for cancer prevention in this demographic.

## English Plain-Language Version

先说结论： Diabetes and pre-diabetes are associated with an increased risk of cancer, particularly obesity-related cancers, in Hispanic/Latino adults.

Why it's relevant: Managing blood sugar levels could be crucial for cancer prevention in the Hispanic/Latino community.

What the evidence tells us: The study involved over 16,000 participants and found higher hazard ratios for cancer among those with pre-diabetes or diabetes.

How to understand it: The link between diabetes and cancer risk suggests broader health benefits of managing diabetes.

What can be done: For readers with prediabetes: Consider lifestyle changes to prevent or manage diabetes. For the health tech industry: Develop tools to help manage diabetes and reduce cancer risk.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

This study is particularly relevant for Hispanic/Latino adults with prediabetes or diabetes, as it suggests that managing these conditions could have implications beyond just blood sugar control, potentially reducing the risk of cancer.

Practical Takeaways

- Diabetes and pre-diabetes are linked to an increased risk of cancer in Hispanic/Latino adults.
- The study involved over 16,000 participants and followed them for an average of 10.7 years.
- Pre-diabetes was associated with a hazard ratio of 1.82 for cancer, and diabetes with a ratio of 2.49.
- Managing diabetes could potentially reduce the risk of cancer, especially obesity-related cancers.
