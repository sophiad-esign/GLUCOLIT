## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Can the Body Mass Index (BMI)-Glucose (ByG) index identify the risk of dysglycemia in patients with hypertension and obstructive sleep apnea?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 1,759 adults with hypertension and OSA from the Urumqi Research on Sleep Apnea and Hypertension study (UROSAH)
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Assessment of the ByG index in identifying the risk of dysglycemia, diabetes, and prediabetes
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of the ByG index with traditional insulin resistance indicators
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** During a median follow-up of 7.25 years, 212 cases of dysglycemia were identified
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Participants in the highest ByG tertile had a significantly increased risk of dysglycemia, diabetes, and prediabetes compared to the lowest tertile
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is retrospective and further validation through prospective studies is needed
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** The ByG index may serve as an early predictor of dysglycemia risk, but its application should be discussed with healthcare providers and does not replace the need for a healthy lifestyle.

## 原文精华摘要

想象一下，如果每天醒来都精神焕发，这可能是控制血糖的一个步骤。 对于糖尿病前期患者来说，睡眠质量可能和他们吃的食物一样重要。 最近的一项研究观察了体重指数（BMI）-血糖（ByG）指数，这可能有助于识别高血压和睡眠呼吸暂停患者发展糖代谢异常的风险。

研究发现，较高的ByG指数与糖代谢异常的更大风险相关。 这个指数可能是一个简单的早期检测工具，但需要更多的研究。 与此同时，优先考虑良好的睡眠习惯可能是管理糖尿病前期的一个实际步骤。

ByG指数是使用个人的BMI和空腹血糖水平计算的。 研究表明，与最低三分位数的人相比，处于最高三分位数的人糖代谢异常的风险显著增加。 这表明ByG指数可能是未来血糖问题的有用预测器。 然而，重要的是要记住，这不是一个确定的测试，饮食和运动等其他因素也起着至关重要的作用。

当谈到睡眠时，质量与数量同样重要。 良好的夜间睡眠可以帮助调节血糖水平，降低患糖尿病的风险。 如果你经常在白天感到疲倦或有入睡困难，这可能是睡眠障碍的迹象。 建立规律的睡眠时间表和创造一个宁静的环境可以改善睡眠质量。

ByG指数提供了早期检测糖代谢异常的一个潜在新工具，但它不是健康生活方式的替代品。 保持均衡饮食、定期运动和良好的睡眠习惯仍然很重要。 与你的医疗服务提供者讨论ByG指数可以提供更个性化的风险见解。

总之，良好的睡眠是管理糖尿病前期的关键组成部分。 ByG指数可能是一个有用的早期警告信号，但包括饮食、运动和睡眠在内的全面方法对于整体健康至关重要。

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine waking up refreshed every day could be a step towards better blood sugar control. For those with prediabetes, the quality of sleep might be as important as the food they eat.

A recent study looked into the Body Mass Index (BMI)-Glucose (ByG) index, which could help identify the risk of developing dysglycemia, including diabetes and prediabetes, in people with hypertension and sleep apnea.

The study found that a higher ByG index was linked to a greater risk of dysglycemia. This index could be a simple tool for early detection, but more research is needed.

In the meantime, prioritizing good sleep habits might be a practical step for those managing prediabetes.

The ByG index is calculated using a person's BMI and fasting blood sugar levels. The study showed that those in the highest tertile of the ByG index had a significantly increased risk of dysglycemia compared to those in the lowest tertile.

This suggests that the ByG index could be a useful predictor of future blood sugar problems. However, it's important to remember that this is not a definitive test, and other factors like diet and exercise also play a crucial role.

When it comes to sleep, quality is just as important as quantity. A good night's sleep can help regulate blood sugar levels and reduce the risk of diabetes.

If you often feel tired during the day or have trouble falling asleep, it might be a sign of a sleep disorder. Establishing a regular sleep schedule and creating a restful environment can improve sleep quality.

The ByG index offers a potential new tool in the early detection of dysglycemia, but it's not a replacement for a healthy lifestyle. It's still important to maintain a balanced diet, regular exercise, and good sleep habits.

Discussing the ByG index with your healthcare provider can provide more personalized insights into your risk.

In summary, good sleep is a key component in managing prediabetes. The ByG index might be a helpful early warning sign, but a comprehensive approach that includes diet, exercise, and sleep is essential for overall health.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Sleep plays a crucial role in managing prediabetes and the ByG index offers a potential new tool for early detection of dysglycemia risk, which is particularly relevant for patients with hypertension and sleep apnea.
