## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What are the metabolic predictors of hippocampal insulin sensitivity, particularly in relation to age and sex?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 260 non-diabetic participants (165 women, mean BMI 29.7 ± 6.2 kg/m2, mean age 44.2 ± 16.6 years)
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Assessment of hippocampal insulin response before and after intranasal insulin administration using functional MRI, metabolic phenotyping including oral glucose tolerance tests, whole-body MRI, and 1H-MRS.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Participants were assigned to high- and low-risk prediabetes clusters using the Tübingen risk cluster tool.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Fasting plasma glucose levels predicted lower hippocampal insulin response with age independently of sex. Significant interactions were present between age, sex, and body fat distribution.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Unhealthy body fat distribution was a sex-dependent predictor for decreased hippocampal insulin sensitivity with increasing age, particularly in older women with high abdominal fat.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study's sample size and specific focus on non-diabetic participants may limit the generalizability of the findings.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes, particularly older women, maintaining a healthy body fat distribution and insulin sensitivity through diet and exercise may be crucial for brain health and the prevention of metabolic and neurodegenerative diseases.

## English Plain-Language Version

Family dinners are a time to make healthier food choices. Recent findings suggest that diet impacts not just our waistlines but also our brain's sensitivity to insulin, crucial for metabolism and memory.

A balanced diet and regular exercise can help maintain insulin sensitivity, especially for those with prediabetes. Unhealthy fat distribution, particularly around the abdomen, is linked to a decline in the brain's insulin response.

Older women with high abdominal fat are more vulnerable to impaired insulin responsiveness in the brain. Small, sustainable lifestyle changes can have a significant impact on long-term health.

Making healthier food choices and incorporating regular physical activity are practical steps towards improving brain insulin sensitivity.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the link between diet, exercise, and brain insulin sensitivity is crucial for individuals with prediabetes, as it provides actionable insights into how lifestyle changes can directly impact their health and potentially reduce

the risk of developing type 2 diabetes and neurodegenerative diseases.
