## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Does weight loss through a dietitian-supported program improve financial outcomes and workplace experiences for adults with prediabetes and overweight or obesity?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 281 adults with prediabetes and a BMI of ≥ 25 kg/m2.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Dietitian-led weight loss counseling over 6 months, followed by 6 months of maintenance.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Baseline data compared to data collected at 6 and 12 months.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Income, employment status, workplace experiences, and BMI.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Modest weight loss over 12 months did not improve income or employment outcomes. There were sex disparities in income that were not altered by weight loss.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study's limitations include a relatively short intervention period and potential for confounding factors not accounted for in the study design.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While weight loss is beneficial for health, it may not directly translate to improved workplace experiences or financial outcomes. Broader societal and structural approaches are needed to address economic disadvantages linked to obesity.

## English Plain-Language Version

Managing prediabetes and weight at work can be challenging. A study explored if weight loss could improve job situations. It involved 281 participants with prediabetes who received dietitian-led weight loss counseling.

After a year, they lost some weight, but this didn't lead to better income or job outcomes. It's important to understand that while weight loss is beneficial for health, it may not directly translate to workplace gains.

This doesn't mean efforts to improve diet and health are wasted; they're crucial. But for broader changes, we need societal support. When considering diet, think about realistic meal plans and portion control.

For exercise, consistency and safety matter. And for sleep, maintaining a regular schedule is key. These are all part of a holistic approach to managing prediabetes.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the relationship between weight loss, diet, and workplace experiences is crucial for individuals with prediabetes who are seeking to manage their health and improve their quality of life.

Practical Takeaways

- Weight loss alone may not improve workplace outcomes for individuals with prediabetes.
- Diet and lifestyle changes are still crucial for health, even if they don't directly impact job situations.
- Societal and structural support is needed to address the broader economic disadvantages linked to obesity.
- Realistic meal plans, portion control, and regular exercise are part of a holistic approach to managing prediabetes.
- Maintaining a regular sleep schedule is important for overall health and can support prediabetes management.
