## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Does concurrent training with a paleolithic-type diet improve physiological responses and lipid dynamics in individuals with type 2 diabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Individuals with overweight or obesity who had type 2 diabetes (n=27)
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** 12 weeks of paleolithic-type diet (PD) or PD and supervised concurrent exercise training three times weekly (PD+E)
- **对照 / Comparison:** PD vs PD+E
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Physiological and glycaemic responses to exercise and recovery, lipid profiles analyzed using mass spectrometry-based lipidomics
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Both groups showed improved glycaemic management, but only PD+E group showed improvements in cardiometabolic fitness. PD+E group also showed enhanced clearance of bioactive lipids from circulation during recovery.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study does not establish causal mechanisms and the sample size was relatively small.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes or early metabolic risk, combining a paleolithic-type diet with regular exercise may lead to improved metabolic health and better management of blood sugar levels.

## English Plain-Language Version

Embarking on a journey to better health, exercise serves as the map.

For those with prediabetes or early metabolic risk, a study indicates that combining a paleolithic-type diet with regular exercise can significantly enhance the body's ability to manage fat and sugar.

This approach not only aids in weight loss but also boosts natural processes for blood sugar control and cardiometabolic fitness.

The study revealed that individuals following this combined method showed improved lipid clearance post-exercise, linked to better fat oxidation during exercise, which is vital for managing insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes.

It's important to consult healthcare providers before starting any new diet or exercise regimen.

The clear message is that for managing prediabetes or early metabolic risk, a combination of a paleolithic-type diet and regular exercise may be a powerful tool.
