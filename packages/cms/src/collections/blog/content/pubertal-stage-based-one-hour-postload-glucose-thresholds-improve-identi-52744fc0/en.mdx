## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Can one-hour postload plasma glucose (1h-PG) thresholds be adjusted for age and pubertal stage to better identify prediabetes and type 2 diabetes in overweight or obese children?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 4344 children and adolescents with overweight or obesity who underwent an oral glucose tolerance test.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Participants were classified into groups based on normal glucose tolerance with normal 1h-PG, normal glucose tolerance with elevated 1h-PG, prediabetes, and type 2 diabetes.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of diagnostic performance between age-based and pubertal stage-based 1h-PG thresholds.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Insulin resistance and β-cell function indices were assessed, and 1h-PG levels were analyzed in relation to pubertal progression.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Pubertal stage-specific thresholds showed superior discrimination compared with age-based stratification and adult cut-offs. A prepubertal cut-off of 7.78 mmol/L improved sensitivity for prediabetes, and a pubertal cut-off of 10.48 mmol/L improved sensitivity for type 2 diabetes.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is cross-sectional and specific to children with overweight or obesity, limiting generalizability.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For readers with prediabetes or early metabolic risk, especially those with children, understanding that standard adult glucose thresholds may not apply can lead to more accurate screening and earlier intervention.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine a child who just had a birthday and is growing fast. Their body is changing, and so is their metabolism. This means that the usual adult guidelines for detecting high blood sugar might not work well for them.

A new study has found that using age- and puberty-specific glucose levels can help identify children at risk of prediabetes more accurately.

This is important because catching prediabetes early can help prevent type 2 diabetes later in life. The study involved over 4,000 children and found that the usual adult thresholds for high blood sugar were not the best for kids.

Instead, they found that different thresholds based on puberty stages worked better. For example, a prepubertal child might be considered at risk if their blood sugar is above 7.

78 mmol/L, while a pubertal child might be at risk if it's above 10. 48 mmol/L. These findings suggest that doctors should consider a child's development stage when checking for prediabetes.

It's not about giving medical advice, but it does mean that if you have concerns about your child's health, it's worth discussing these new findings with a healthcare provider.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Detecting prediabetes early is crucial for preventing the progression to type 2 diabetes, especially in children who are at a higher risk due to obesity.

This study provides a more nuanced approach to identifying prediabetes in children by considering the changes in insulin sensitivity that occur during puberty.

Practical Takeaways

- Puberty affects how a child's body handles sugar, so one-size-fits-all glucose thresholds don't work.
- New puberty-specific thresholds can help identify prediabetes in children more accurately.
- Doctors should consider a child's development stage when checking for prediabetes.
- Discuss these findings with a healthcare provider if you have concerns about your child's health.
- Early detection of prediabetes can help prevent type 2 diabetes in the future.
