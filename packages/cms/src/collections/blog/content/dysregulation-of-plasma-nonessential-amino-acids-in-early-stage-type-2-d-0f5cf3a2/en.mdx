## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How do nonessential amino acid levels vary in prediabetes and early-stage type 2 diabetes, and how do these levels relate to obesity and insulin resistance?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 180 individuals categorized as healthy controls, prediabetes, and T2DM.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Measurement of nonessential amino acids, glucose, HbA1c, and insulin in participants.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of amino acid levels between healthy controls, prediabetes, and T2DM patients.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Newly diagnosed T2DM patients had increased levels of alanine, asparagine, aspartate, glutamate, and proline but decreased levels of serine, arginine, cystine, glutamine, and glycine.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Altered amino acid metabolism is associated with prediabetes and early T2DM, influenced by obesity and correlating with insulin resistance and BMI.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study's findings are associative and do not establish causation. The sample size is relatively small, and the study population may not be fully representative.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While this study suggests a link between amino acid levels and metabolic health, it does not provide a medical prescription. Readers with prediabetes or early diabetes should discuss dietary changes with a healthcare provider to manage their condition effectively.

## English Plain-Language Version

At a dinner party, a friend mentions having prediabetes. You share that managing diet, especially amino acid balance, could slow diabetes progression. This isn't medical advice; it's a conversation starter with healthcare providers.

Amino acids, the building blocks of proteins, signal metabolic health. In prediabetes and early diabetes, certain nonessential amino acid levels shift, indicating how the body handles sugars and fats.

For instance, higher alanine, asparagine, and aspartate levels are linked to higher BMI and insulin resistance. Conversely, lower serine, arginine, and glycine levels are observed.

A balanced diet with complex carbs, lean proteins, and various fruits and vegetables can subtly adjust amino acid balance, potentially influencing insulin sensitivity and glucose control.

Remember, diet is part of a holistic approach that includes exercise, sleep, and medical advice. Always consult healthcare providers before making significant dietary changes.

Managing amino acid levels through diet supports metabolic health by focusing on nutrient quality and interactions within the body's complex systems.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding amino acids' role in metabolic health is vital for those with prediabetes or early diabetes. Amino acid levels can indicate disease progression and guide management.

Adjusting diet to influence these levels may improve metabolic health and delay diabetes onset.

Practical Takeaways

- Diet influences amino acid levels linked to prediabetes and early diabetes.
- Complex carbs, lean proteins, and a variety of fruits and vegetables help maintain amino acid balance.
- High alanine, asparagine, aspartate levels are associated with higher BMI and insulin resistance.
- Low serine, arginine, glycine levels are observed in prediabetes and early diabetes.
- Consult healthcare providers before making significant dietary changes.
