## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Does gut microbiota-generated TMAO from dietary choline affect insulin sensitivity and glucose tolerance in humans?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Healthy, sedentary humans aged 35 ± 16 years for the acute study and 46 ± 14 years for the short-term study.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Consumption of 1,000 mg/day choline bitartrate and placebo (maltodextrin) the night before each testing session.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Plasma TMAO levels and insulin sensitivity (Matsuda index), insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), fasting glucose, 2-h glucose, area under the curve, and incremental area under the curve were compared between the choline and placebo conditions.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Plasma TMAO increased by 400% after acute study and 110% after 4 weeks of daily ingestion of 1,000 mg choline bitartrate. No significant differences in insulin sensitivity, insulin resistance, or glucose tolerance were found.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Acute and short-term dietary choline increased TMAO without affecting insulin sensitivity, glucose tolerance, or fasting glucose and insulin.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was limited to short-term effects and a specific population, and causality cannot be established.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While dietary choline can increase TMAO levels, this study suggests that it may not directly impact insulin sensitivity or glucose tolerance in the short term. However, the relationship between TMAO and glucose homeostasis requires further investigation.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're at a dinner party, and you're trying to make healthier choices. You might be surprised to learn that the food you choose could affect your body's ability to process sugar.

A recent study looked at how a nutrient called choline, found in foods like eggs and liver, might impact your blood sugar levels.

The researchers found that while choline can increase a substance in your body called TMAO, it doesn't seem to affect your body's sensitivity to insulin or how well it handles sugar.

This is important because high levels of TMAO have been linked to type 2 diabetes, but this study suggests that the relationship might not be as straightforward as once thought.

It's a reminder that what we eat can have complex effects on our health, and more research is needed to fully understand these connections.

For now, if you're managing prediabetes, it's still a good idea to talk to your doctor about a balanced diet that includes a variety of nutrients.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the impact of diet on gut microbiota and glucose homeostasis is crucial for individuals with prediabetes, as it can influence their risk of developing type 2 diabetes and guide dietary interventions.

Practical Takeaways

- Dietary choline can increase TMAO levels in the body.
- TMAO has been linked to type 2 diabetes, but this study suggests it may not directly cause insulin resistance or affect glucose tolerance.
- More research is needed to fully understand the relationship between TMAO, diet, and glucose homeostasis.
- It's important for individuals with prediabetes to discuss dietary choices with their healthcare provider.
