## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Does intermittent energy restriction, exercise, or their combination, improve glycemic control and reduce body weight in individuals with type 2 diabetes not treated with insulin?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Adults aged 18 to 75 years with type 2 diabetes, BMI >27 kg/m2, HbA1c between 53 mmol/mol and 86 mmol/mol, and not on insulin therapy.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Participants are randomly allocated to one of four groups: intermittent energy restriction, exercise, combined intermittent energy restriction and exercise, or a control group.
- **对照 / Comparison:** The study compares the effects of the three interventions against a control group over a 12-week period.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Primary outcome is the change in body weight from baseline to day 84. Secondary outcomes include changes in glycemic parameters, resting energy expenditure, body composition, and continuous glucose monitoring results.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** The study is ongoing, and findings are not yet available. However, it aims to assess the safety and efficacy of the interventions in improving glycemic control and reducing body weight.
- **局限性 / Limits:** As the study is still in progress, no conclusions can be drawn yet. The study's results will be limited to the population studied and may not be generalizable to all individuals with type 2 diabetes.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** Readers with prediabetes or early metabolic risks can look forward to the study's results for potential lifestyle interventions. However, it's important to remember that individual responses to diet and exercise can vary, and what works best should be discussed with a healthcare provider.

该研究涉及没有使用胰岛素的2型糖尿病患者，分为四组：一组进行间歇性能量限制，一组进行运动，一组两者都做，还有一组作为对照组。 这个为期12周的干预旨在评估这些方法如何影响体重和血糖控制。 它寻求了解什么对不同个体最有效。

运动扮演着关键角色，不仅仅是燃烧卡路里，还在于它如何与胰岛素敏感性相互作用。 研究既着眼于即时效果，也着眼于长期益处，包括身体组成和能量消耗的变化。

虽然这些发现很有希望，但它们并不是放之四海而皆准的处方。 每个人的身体对饮食和运动的反应都不同。 重要的是找到一个适合你的健康目标，并且你可以坚持的计划。

## English Plain-Language Version

Managing blood sugar is like juggling. For those with prediabetes or early metabolic risks, lifestyle changes can make a big difference.

A study, INTERFAST-3, is exploring how intermittent fasting and regular exercise might help manage type 2 diabetes. It's about more than just weight loss; it's about improving insulin sensitivity and keeping blood sugar stable.

The study involves people with type 2 diabetes not on insulin, divided into four groups: one for intermittent energy restriction, one for exercise, one combining both, and a control group.

The 12-week intervention aims to assess effects on body weight and blood sugar control. It seeks to understand what works best for different individuals.

Exercise plays a key role, not just in burning calories but also in how it interacts with insulin sensitivity. The study looks at immediate and long-term benefits, including changes in body composition and energy expenditure.

While these findings are promising, they're not a one-size-fits-all prescription. Everyone's body responds differently to diet and exercise. What's important is finding a plan that fits your health goals and that you can stick with.

The INTERFAST-3 study is ongoing, but its insights can guide us towards more personalized lifestyle interventions for type 2 diabetes. It's about living a healthier, more active life, not just about numbers on a scale or a blood test.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Managing type 2 diabetes is not just about medication; lifestyle changes play a crucial role. Understanding how diet and exercise can interact with insulin resistance is key for individuals looking to improve their health outcomes.

Practical Takeaways

- Intermittent fasting and regular exercise may help manage type 2 diabetes by improving insulin sensitivity and blood sugar control.
- The INTERFAST-3 study is exploring the safety and efficacy of these lifestyle changes in individuals not on insulin.
- Findings from the study can guide personalized lifestyle interventions for better diabetes management.
- It's important to find a sustainable plan that works for your health goals and is tailored to your body's response.
