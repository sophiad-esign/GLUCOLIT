## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How does diet relate to the gut microbiome in adults with prediabetes and obesity?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Adults with prediabetes and obesity enrolled in The Personal Diet study.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Analysis of the relationship between gut microbiome and dietary intake, body composition, glycemic measures, and self-efficacy.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of gut bacterial diversity and abundances associated with host and lifestyle variables.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Associations between dietary factors and gut microbial beta diversity, and the Prevotella/Bacteroides ratio with total energy, sugar, and carbohydrate intake.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Dietary factors were the most consistent correlates of gut microbial beta diversity. Higher intake of sugars, carbohydrates, and total energy was associated with a less diverse gut microbiome, while monounsaturated fat intake was associated with a healthier gut environment.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is a post-hoc cross-sectional analysis with a specific population, limiting generalizability. Causality cannot be inferred from these associations.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes and obesity, dietary choices have a significant impact on gut microbiome diversity, which in turn may influence metabolic health. This suggests that a diet rich in monounsaturated fats and low in sugars and carbohydrates could be beneficial for managing prediabetes and obesity.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're managing prediabetes and obesity. Your diet isn't just about what you like; it shapes your gut's microbial community, affecting your health.

A recent study shows that dietary factors are the most consistent correlates of gut microbial diversity. This means the foods you eat can either support or disrupt your metabolic balance.

For instance, a higher intake of sugars and carbohydrates is linked to a less diverse gut microbiome, which can contribute to insulin resistance. On the other hand, a diet rich in monounsaturated fats may foster a healthier gut environment.

Understanding these links can help you make dietary choices that not only satisfy your taste buds but also support your metabolic health.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the relationship between diet and the gut microbiome is crucial for individuals with prediabetes and obesity because it provides a tangible way to influence metabolic health and potentially prevent the progression to type 2

diabetes.

Practical Takeaways

- Diet significantly influences gut microbiome diversity.
- A diet high in sugars and carbohydrates may negatively impact gut health.
- Intake of monounsaturated fats is associated with a healthier gut microbiome.
- Making informed dietary choices can support metabolic health in prediabetes and obesity.
