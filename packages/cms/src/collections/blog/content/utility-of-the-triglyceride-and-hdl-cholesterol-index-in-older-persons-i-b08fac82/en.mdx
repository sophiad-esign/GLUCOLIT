## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the association and predictive capacity of the TG/HDL-c index for cardiometabolic risk factors in older adults?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Adults aged 60 years and above from the national SABE survey in Colombia.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** The TG/HDL-c index was used to assess cardiovascular disease risk.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Univariable and multivariable logistic regression models were constructed to examine associations between the TG/HDL-c index and diabetes or metabolic syndrome, adjusting for age, sex, BMI, smoking, diet, and physical activity.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** The TG/HDL-c index was independently associated with both diabetes and metabolic syndrome.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** The TG/HDL-c index demonstrated a good discriminatory performance for diabetes (AUC = 0.89) and metabolic syndrome (AUC = 0.86), with specific cut-off values for each.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was cross-sectional, limiting the ability to establish causation. The discriminatory performance of the TG/HDL-c index was limited for hypertension, prediabetes, cardiovascular disease, and cerebrovascular disease.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** The TG/HDL-c index may be a useful tool for identifying older adults at higher cardiometabolic risk, but its predictive value for other conditions is less clear. Further longitudinal studies are needed to validate these findings.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're at a family dinner, surrounded by loved ones and a spread of delicious foods. But with prediabetes, every meal choice matters.

A recent study from Colombia sheds light on how the balance of fats in our blood, specifically the triglyceride-to-HDL cholesterol (TG/HDL-c) index, can signal our risk for diabetes and metabolic syndrome.

This index could be a simple way to gauge if our diet and lifestyle are on track.

The TG/HDL-c index is a ratio that measures the balance of two types of blood fats. High levels of triglycerides and low levels of HDL cholesterol are linked to an increased risk of heart disease and metabolic issues.

In the study, researchers found that this index was strongly associated with diabetes and metabolic syndrome in older adults, suggesting it could be a useful tool for identifying those at higher risk.

But what does this mean for you? It's not just about the numbers; it's about the lifestyle changes we can make.

A diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains, along with regular physical activity, can help improve this index and reduce our risk of developing更严重的 health problems.

It's important to remember that while the TG/HDL-c index is a helpful indicator, it's not a definitive diagnosis. It's one piece of the puzzle in managing our health.

And as with any health information, it's crucial to discuss any changes with a healthcare provider before making drastic alterations to your lifestyle.

In summary, the TG/HDL-c index provides a snapshot of our metabolic health, and by making mindful choices about what we eat and how we move, we can take steps towards better health.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Managing prediabetes is crucial for preventing the progression to type 2 diabetes and reducing the risk of cardiovascular diseases.

Understanding and improving the TG/HDL-c index can provide a practical approach to monitoring and managing metabolic health.

Practical Takeaways

- The TG/HDL-c index can be a useful tool for assessing cardiometabolic risk, especially in older adults.
- A balanced diet with plenty of fruits, vegetables, and whole grains can help improve the TG/HDL-c index.
- Regular physical activity is an important part of managing prediabetes and improving metabolic health.
- The TG/HDL-c index is not a definitive diagnosis but a guide to help understand metabolic health.
- Discuss any significant lifestyle changes with a healthcare provider to ensure they are safe and appropriate for your individual health needs.
