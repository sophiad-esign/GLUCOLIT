## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What dietary patterns are associated with metabolic syndrome and insulin resistance among U.S. women?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 4,426 women aged ≥18 years from the 2013-2023 National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Dietary patterns, including low-carb, high-fiber, and high-monounsaturated fat intake
- **对照 / Comparison:** Temporal trends and dietary associations were examined using survey-weighted logistic regression and generalized structural equation modeling
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Metabolic syndrome prevalence, elevated HbA1c, hyperinsulinemia, and insulin resistance
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Low-carb diets were associated with lower odds of elevated HbA1c and hyperinsulinemia. Higher fiber and monounsaturated fatty acid intakes were inversely associated with MetS and insulin-related markers.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study suggests associations that can guide further discussions with healthcare providers. The sample is specific to U.S. women and may not be generalizable to other populations.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** These findings should not be taken as personal medical advice. Readers should consider discussing these dietary patterns with healthcare providers to determine what might be best for their individual health needs.

## English Plain-Language Version

Choosing the right foods can help manage blood sugar levels. A diet rich in fiber and monounsaturated fats is linked to better metabolic health.

This suggests that whole grains, fruits, and vegetables could be healthier choices than refined carbs.

Metabolic syndrome affects about 20% of women, with insulin resistance affecting over 40%. The risk increases with later stages of menopause.

A diet lower in carbs and higher in fiber, monounsaturated fats, and protein may offer some protection.

It's important to consider the quality of protein and fats in our diet. A diet with a better omega-6:omega-3 ratio could be beneficial, suggesting more fish and less processed food high in omega-6 fats.

A diet rich in fiber, monounsaturated fats, and protein, with a lower omega-6:omega-3 ratio, could support better metabolic health. Discussing dietary changes with a healthcare provider is always wise.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the dietary patterns that could influence metabolic health is crucial, especially for women, as metabolic syndrome and insulin resistance are common and can lead to serious health complications.

Practical Takeaways

- A diet with higher fiber and monounsaturated fats may be associated with improved metabolic health in women.
- Insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome are prevalent, especially affecting women during later menopausal stages.
- The balance of omega-6 to omega-3 fatty acids in diet plays a significant role in metabolic health.
- Discussing dietary changes with healthcare providers ensures they are personalized and safe.
