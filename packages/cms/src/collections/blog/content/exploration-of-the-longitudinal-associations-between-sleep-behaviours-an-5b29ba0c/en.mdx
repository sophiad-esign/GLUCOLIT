## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What are the associations between sleep behaviors, anthropometric measures, and body composition markers in adults with prediabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 118 adults with prediabetes recruited from a healthcare clinic.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Sleep behaviors assessed using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) and the Malay-translated Munich Chronotype Questionnaire (MCTQ).
- **对照 / Comparison:** Six-month longitudinal study with dietary counseling.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Anthropometric measurements and body composition markers.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Later sleep midpoints associated with greater weight changes (β: 0.47 kg, 95% CI: 0.07, 0.86). Later awake times associated with greater reduction in fat-free mass (β: -0.86 kg, 95%CI: -1.29, -0.43).
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study shows associations but does not establish causation. The sample size and duration limit the generalizability of the findings.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While this study provides insights into the relationship between sleep behaviors and metabolic health in prediabetes, it should be considered a starting point for further investigation and not a definitive guide for clinical practice.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine trying to keep a garden healthy without sunlight or water – that's similar to managing prediabetes without considering sleep. Sleep is a vital component of metabolic health, and new research highlights its importance.

A recent study involving 118 adults with prediabetes found that later sleep midpoints were associated with greater weight changes, while later awake times were linked to a reduction in fat-free mass.

This suggests that the timing of sleep could significantly impact metabolic markers in prediabetes. Understanding these associations can help us design more effective lifestyle interventions.

It's not just about how long you sleep, but also when you sleep. Quality also matters; poor sleep can disrupt metabolic regulation, similar to how a poor diet can.

By focusing on sleep schedules, duration, and quality, we can better manage prediabetes and improve overall health. It's important to note that while this study provides valuable insights, it doesn't prove causation.

The associations found are a starting point for further exploration and should guide future research and interventions.

Practical steps include discussing sleep patterns with healthcare providers and considering how changes in sleep habits might fit into a broader lifestyle plan.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Sleep is often overlooked in discussions about metabolic health, yet it plays a critical role in regulating metabolic processes.

For individuals with prediabetes, understanding the impact of sleep on their condition can lead to more effective self-management strategies and lifestyle changes.

Practical Takeaways

- Sleep timing and quality are crucial for metabolic health in prediabetes.
- Later sleep midpoints and awake times can impact weight and fat-free mass.
- Consider discussing sleep patterns with healthcare providers as part of lifestyle management.
- Lifestyle interventions for prediabetes should include sleep behavior considerations.
- Further research is needed to establish causation and develop targeted interventions.
