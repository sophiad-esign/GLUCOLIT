## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the efficacy of pro-, pre-, and postbiotics in modulating the gut-adipose tissue axis in overweight dogs or diet-induced obesity models?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Overweight dogs (BCS ≥ 6/9) or diet-induced obesity models.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Administration of pro-, pre-, and postbiotics.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of effects between different strains of postbiotics and probiotics.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Postprandial glucose AUC, weight gain, fasting glucose, insulin resistance indexes, circulating adiponectin, and lipid profiles.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Postbiotic Bifidobacterium animalis subsp. lactis CECT 8145 reduced postprandial glucose AUC by 6% during energy restriction. Pasteurized Akkermansia muciniphila postbiotics limited diet-induced weight gain with strain-specific glucoregulatory impacts. Specific probiotics reduced fasting hyperinsulinemia and preserved adiponectin, but effects on lipid profiles were inconsistent.
- **局限性 / Limits:** Lack of standardized reporting for species-validated insulin sensitivity metrics and high-certainty evidence.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While microbiome-targeted therapies show potential in managing obesity and metabolic disorders in dogs, their efficacy is strain-specific and dependent on energy balance. Further research with standardized measures is needed.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine your dog, once energetic, now struggles to keep up with you on walks. This could be a sign of obesity affecting their metabolic health.

A systematic review points to the potential of pro-, pre-, and postbiotics in managing this issue. In overweight dogs, postbiotic Bifidobacterium animalis subsp.

lactis CECT 8145 was found to reduce post-meal glucose levels by 6% during calorie restriction.

Meanwhile, pasteurized Akkermansia muciniphila postbiotics showed promise in limiting weight gain, with varying effects on glucose regulation depending on the strain.

Certain probiotics also helped in reducing fasting hyperinsulinemia and maintaining adiponectin levels, though effects on lipid profiles were inconsistent.

It's important to note that the relationship between microbial metabolites and incretin signaling is not fully understood, and there's a lack of standardized measures for insulin sensitivity in dogs.

While microbiome-targeted therapies show potential, their efficacy is strain-specific and dependent on energy balance.

For pet owners, this means that dietary management and the inclusion of specific probiotics could be part of a strategy to support your dog's metabolic health.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Managing obesity and metabolic disorders in pets is crucial for their long-term health and well-being. Understanding the role of diet and microbiome modulation can help pet owners make informed decisions about their pet's care.

Practical Takeaways

- Diet and specific probiotics can play a role in managing obesity and metabolic health in dogs.
- The effects of postbiotics on glucose regulation can be strain-specific and dependent on energy balance.
- Inconsistent effects on lipid profiles were observed across different trials.
- Microbiome-targeted therapies show promise but require further research with standardized measures.
