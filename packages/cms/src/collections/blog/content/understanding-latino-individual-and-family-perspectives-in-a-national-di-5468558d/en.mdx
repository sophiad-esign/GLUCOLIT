- **研究问题 / Research question:** What are the perceived individual and family barriers and facilitators to lifestyle change for Latino/a adult participants in the National Diabetes Prevention Program?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Spanish-speaking Latino/a adults who participated in a community-based NDPP in Colorado.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Participation in the National Diabetes Prevention Program.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of cultural and family factors influencing diabetes prevention behaviors.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Themes and subthemes reflecting individual and family support perspectives.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Cultural biases and gender roles as barriers; family support and culturally aligned programs as facilitators.
- **局限性 / Limits:** Qualitative study with a small sample size, limiting generalizability.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** Understanding these cultural and family dynamics can help in designing more effective diabetes prevention programs tailored to Latino communities.

  English Plain-Language Version
  Latino adults in the US face higher risks of developing type 2 diabetes and are less likely to meet the goals of the National Diabetes Prevention Program (NDPP) compared to non-Latino White adults.

Understanding the cultural and family dynamics that influence their participation is crucial.

What the evidence says The study identified cultural biases and gender roles as barriers, while family support and culturally aligned programs were facilitators for diabetes prevention among Latino/a adults.

What this means The study underscores the significance of cultural nuances and family involvement in diabetes prevention. It suggests that tailored, culturally sensitive programs could enhance effectiveness.

What readers can do Culturally sensitive programs that include family support may improve diabetes prevention outcomes among Latino communities. Health tech can play a role by developing relevant solutions.
This study is relevant because it provides insights into how cultural and family factors can either hinder or facilitate diabetes prevention efforts among a high-risk population, offering practical implications for improving prevention

programs.

- Cultural and family factors significantly influence diabetes prevention behaviors among Latino adults.
- Programs that consider cultural nuances and involve families may be more effective for diabetes prevention.
- Understanding one's own cultural biases and gender roles can be a barrier to engaging in health behaviors.
- Health tech solutions that are culturally sensitive and family-oriented could improve engagement and outcomes.
