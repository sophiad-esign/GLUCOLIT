## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How does the volume of strength exercise affect glucose metabolism in individuals with obesity and varying levels of insulin resistance?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 15 individuals with obesity (fat percentage and visceral fat content > 90th percentile for age and sex), divided into insulin-sensitive and resistant groups.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Participants underwent three randomized sessions: high-volume (HIGH) strength exercise (8-RM, 21 sets to failure), low-volume (LOW) strength exercise (7 sets to failure), and a control session with no exercise.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparisons were made between the insulin-sensitive and resistant groups after each exercise session.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) was performed to measure insulin sensitivity, glucose, insulin, and C-peptide area under the curve.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Improvements in oral glucose insulin sensitivity and the area under the curve for glucose, insulin, and C-peptide were observed only after HIGH in the resistant group. A higher percentage of responders was also found in the resistant group after HIGH.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study did not find improvements in hepatic insulin resistance, hepatic insulin extraction, and C-peptidogenic index. The sample size was small, and the study was conducted in a specific population of individuals with obesity.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with obesity and insulin resistance, high-volume strength training may be more effective in improving certain aspects of glucose metabolism compared to low-volume training. However, the findings should be discussed with a healthcare provider as they may not apply to all individuals and do not address all aspects of glucose metabolism.

控制血糖水平至关重要，尤其是对于肥胖和胰岛素抵抗的患者。 最近的研究揭示了锻炼量的作用。 与低量训练相比，大量力量训练似乎能改善肥胖和胰岛素抵抗患者的血糖控制。 这一发现很重要，因为它强调了锻炼量的重要性，特别是对于那些对胰岛素更有抵抗力的个体。

研究包括15名肥胖个体，他们根据胰岛素抵抗水平被分类，并接受了大量或少量力量训练，或作为对照组。 大量组进行了21组至力竭的训练，而少量组完成了7组。 进行了口服葡萄糖耐量测试，以评估他们的身体对葡萄糖的反应。

在有胰岛素抵抗的大量组中观察到了显著的改善，表明更好的胰岛素利用和葡萄糖处理。 然而，并非所有血糖代谢方面都显示出改善，例如肝脏胰岛素处理和C肽原指数。 这表明，虽然大量力量训练可以帮助控制某些血糖方面，但它并不是一个全面的解决方案。

## English Plain-Language Version

Managing blood sugar levels is crucial, especially with obesity and insulin resistance. A recent study sheds light on the role of exercise volume.

High-volume strength training appears to improve blood sugar control in those with obesity and insulin resistance, as opposed to low-volume training.

This insight is significant because it highlights the importance of exercise quantity, particularly for individuals more resistant to insulin.

The study included 15 obese individuals, categorized by insulin resistance, who underwent either high-volume or low-volume strength training, or served as a control group.

The high-volume group performed 21 sets to failure, while the low-volume group completed 7 sets. An oral glucose tolerance test was conducted to assess their bodies' response to glucose.

Significant improvements were noted in the high-volume group with insulin resistance, indicating better insulin utilization and glucose processing.

However, not all aspects of glucose metabolism showed improvement, such as liver insulin processing and the C-peptidogenic index.

This suggests that while high-volume strength training can aid in some blood sugar control aspects, it is not a comprehensive solution.

It's essential to remember that exercise can be a powerful tool for managing blood sugar, but the specifics, including quantity and type, are vital.

What works for one person may not for another, emphasizing the need to find the right exercise balance.

Always consult with a healthcare provider to determine the best exercise regimen, especially if you have prediabetes or are at risk for diabetes.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the effects of exercise volume on glucose metabolism is essential for individuals with prediabetes or insulin resistance, guiding them in making informed decisions about their exercise routines.

Practical Takeaways

- High-volume strength training may enhance blood sugar control in individuals with obesity and insulin resistance.
- Exercise quantity is key, potentially benefiting some individuals more with higher volumes.
- Glucose metabolism improvements are not universal; some aspects like liver insulin processing did not improve.
- Consult healthcare providers for personalized exercise plans, especially with prediabetes or diabetes risk.
