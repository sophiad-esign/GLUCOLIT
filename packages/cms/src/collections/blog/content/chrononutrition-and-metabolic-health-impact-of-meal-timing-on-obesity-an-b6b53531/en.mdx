## English Plain-Language Version

Planning meals involves more than just what to eat; when you eat is equally important. Chrononutrition, a field within chronobiology, studies how meal timing interacts with our biological rhythms.

Individuals with prediabetes or early metabolic risk might find that meal timing significantly impacts their health. Those who eat late at night or have extended eating windows may face higher insulin resistance and increased belly fat.

Understanding your body's natural rhythms can guide small adjustments to your eating schedule, potentially improving metabolic health. However, it's crucial to remember that everyone's body responds differently to meal timing.

Practical steps include avoiding late-night eating and aiming to finish meals at least two to three hours before bedtime. A 10 to 12-hour eating window might also be beneficial.

Always consult with a healthcare provider before making dietary changes.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding meal timing's impact on metabolic health is essential for those with prediabetes or early metabolic risk, guiding informed dietary and lifestyle decisions.

Practical Takeaways

- Meal timing can significantly impact metabolic health.
- Avoid late-night eating and consider a shorter eating window.
- Ending meals two to three hours before bedtime can aid digestion.
- A 10 to 12-hour eating window may benefit metabolic health.
- Consult healthcare providers before adjusting your meal schedule.
