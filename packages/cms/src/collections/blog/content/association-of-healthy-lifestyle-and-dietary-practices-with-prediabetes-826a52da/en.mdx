## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the association between healthy lifestyle and dietary practices and prediabetes among young Korean adults?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 3,865 participants (1,805 men and 2,060 women) aged 19-34 from the Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey 2020-2024.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Healthy lifestyle practices included nonsmoking, moderating drinking, and walking. Healthy dietary practices comprised appropriate fat intake, sodium intake of ≤ 2,300 mg/day, fruit and vegetable intake of ≥ 500 g/day, and nutrition label use.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Participants with and without prediabetes were compared based on their lifestyle and dietary practices.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Prediabetes was defined as a fasting plasma glucose level of 100-125 mg/dL or a glycated hemoglobin level of 5.7-6.4% without diagnosed diabetes.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** 15.9% of participants had prediabetes. Men with prediabetes had lower adherence to healthy dietary practices. Women with prediabetes had higher dietary fat density and lower fiber intake density. Adherence to healthy dietary practices was significantly associated with lower odds of prediabetes in men (adjusted OR, 0.72; 95% CI, 0.54-0.96; P-value = 0.025).
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is cross-sectional, limiting causal inferences. The sample is specific to young Korean adults, which may limit generalizability.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For young adults with prediabetes, especially men, adopting healthy dietary practices may reduce the risk of prediabetes. However, the findings should be discussed with a healthcare provider for personalized advice and not treated as prescriptive.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're at a restaurant with friends, enjoying a meal. What you choose to eat could be a step towards managing your prediabetes.

A study of young Korean adults found that certain dietary habits were linked to lower odds of prediabetes in men. This is important because prediabetes increases the risk of developing type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

The study didn't find a link between general lifestyle habits like smoking and drinking, and prediabetes, but diet seemed to matter. Specifically, men who followed healthy dietary practices were less likely to have prediabetes.

For women, it was about avoiding high-fat and low-fiber diets. These findings suggest that making certain dietary changes early on could help manage metabolic risks.

It's not about drastic changes, but small, sustainable steps towards healthier eating.

When it comes to diet, think quality over quantity. For men, using nutrition labels and not eating out too often seemed to help. For women, it was about getting enough fiber and keeping fat intake in check.

These are actionable steps that can be discussed with a healthcare provider to find what works best for you. Remember, this is not medical advice, but a reflection on the study's findings.

It's about understanding how our daily choices can impact our health.

The study also reminds us that our eating habits can signal our health. If you often eat out or skip checking nutrition labels, it might be time to reconsider. For women, a diet high in fat and low in fiber could be a red flag.

These are signs that could indicate a higher risk of prediabetes. It's important to pay attention to these signals and take action.

In the end, the study suggests that managing prediabetes is not just about individual willpower but also about the choices we make every day.

It's about finding a balance that works for you, whether it's choosing a healthier meal option or being more mindful of your food intake. And it's never too early to start making these changes.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Prediabetes is a significant health concern as it increases the risk of developing type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

Understanding how dietary and lifestyle practices can influence prediabetes is crucial for early intervention and risk management, especially among young adults who are establishing lifelong health behaviors.

Practical Takeaways

- Dietary practices can play a significant role in managing prediabetes, especially among young adults.
- Men who follow healthy dietary practices, such as using nutrition labels and not eating out frequently, have lower odds of prediabetes.
- For women, avoiding diets high in fat and low in fiber can be beneficial in managing prediabetes.
- Small, sustainable dietary changes can make a big difference in metabolic health.
- Early adoption of healthy eating habits may be key to long-term metabolic risk management.
