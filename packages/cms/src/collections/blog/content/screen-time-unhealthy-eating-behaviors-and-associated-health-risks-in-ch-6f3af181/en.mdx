## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What is the relationship between screen time, unhealthy eating behaviors, and health risks in children?
- **研究对象 / Population:** Children and adolescents aged 0-18 years
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Exposure to screen time and digital food marketing
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of dietary behaviors and health outcomes between high and low screen time groups
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Unhealthy eating behaviors, sedentary behavior, sleep disturbances, and mental health issues
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Excessive screen time is associated with unhealthy eating behaviors, sedentary behavior, sleep disturbances, and mental health issues. Digital food marketing influences children's food preferences and intake.
- **局限性 / Limits:** Most evidence is observational, and causal relationships have not been established. Individual responses may vary.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While there is a clear association between screen time and unhealthy behaviors, it's important to approach these findings with caution. Families can take practical steps to reduce screen time and improve dietary habits, but further research is needed to develop targeted interventions.

## English Plain-Language Version

Family dinners with everyone on their devices are becoming common. This raises concerns about how screen time affects children's eating habits and health.

A review finds that excessive screen time is linked to unhealthy eating behaviors, such as increased snacking, skipping breakfast, and a preference for ultra-processed foods.

Children exposed to digital food marketing are more likely to develop poor food preferences. To address these risks, consider setting screen time limits and promoting healthier food choices.

Engaging in family activities that involve physical movement can replace screen time, and preparing meals together can foster discussions on nutrition.

It's important to note that while there's a clear association, causation isn't proven, and individual responses may vary. The review calls for more research to establish causality and develop effective strategies.

In the meantime, small, family-based changes can make a big difference.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Screen time is a significant part of modern childhood, and its impact on diet and health is a pressing concern for parents and educators.

Understanding the link between screen time and eating behaviors can help families make informed decisions about media use and promote healthier lifestyles.

Practical Takeaways

- Set screen time limits to encourage healthier eating and activity.
- Replace screen time with family activities that involve physical movement.
- Prepare meals together to discuss nutrition and make healthier choices.
- Be aware that while screen time is associated with unhealthy eating, more research is needed to establish causation.
- Small changes in family routines can lead to significant health benefits.
