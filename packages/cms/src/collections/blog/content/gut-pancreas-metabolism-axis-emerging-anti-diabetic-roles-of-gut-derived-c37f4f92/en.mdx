## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're at a dinner party, surrounded by delicious food. But if you have prediabetes, you might be worried about what to eat.

The good news is, recent research suggests that certain foods can help improve your insulin sensitivity and glucose metabolism.

The gut-pancreas-metabolism axis is a complex network that includes gut microbiota, which can be influenced by what you eat.

Short-chain fatty acids and other bioactive compounds from the gut can affect insulin secretion and sensitivity, inflammation, and energy metabolism.

This means that by choosing the right foods, you might be able to help your body manage blood sugar levels better. However, it's important to remember that while this is promising, it's not a one-size-fits-all solution.

Everyone's body is different, and what works for one person might not work for another. It's always best to discuss dietary changes with a healthcare provider.

In the meantime, consider incorporating more fiber-rich foods, like vegetables and whole grains, into your diet. These can help feed the good bacteria in your gut that produce beneficial short-chain fatty acids.

But remember, it's not just about what you eat, but also about the balance of your overall diet and lifestyle.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the gut-pancreas-metabolism axis is relevant for individuals with prediabetes or early metabolic risk because it offers a new perspective on how diet can influence glucose metabolism and insulin sensitivity, potentially

leading to more targeted and effective dietary interventions.

Practical Takeaways

- The gut-pancreas-metabolism axis plays a crucial role in glucose homeostasis.
- Dietary changes can influence the gut microbiota and its metabolites, potentially improving insulin sensitivity.
- It's important to discuss dietary changes with a healthcare provider to find a personalized approach.
- Incorporating fiber-rich foods like vegetables and whole grains can help feed beneficial gut bacteria.
