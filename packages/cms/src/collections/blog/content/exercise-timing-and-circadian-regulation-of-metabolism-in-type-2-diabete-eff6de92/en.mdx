## English Plain-Language Version

Waking up to manage blood sugar can be challenging. Quality and timing of sleep significantly affect metabolic health, especially for those with prediabetes or insulin resistance.

Recent findings indicate that exercising in the afternoon or evening might lead to better glycemic control and insulin sensitivity than morning workouts.

However, individual differences matter, and consulting healthcare providers before changing routines is advisable.

Aim for 7-9 hours of sleep per night and maintain a regular sleep-wake cycle for metabolic benefits. If sleep is a struggle, consider a relaxing bedtime routine and avoid caffeine and electronics before bed to enhance sleep quality.

While crucial, exercise and sleep are part of a broader health plan that includes diet and medication when necessary, in collaboration with healthcare teams.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Understanding the role of sleep and circadian rhythms in metabolism is vital, especially for individuals with prediabetes or insulin resistance.

Proper timing of sleep and exercise can lead to better management of these conditions and enhance overall health.

Practical Takeaways

- Circadian rhythm alignment with exercise can improve metabolic health.
- Strive for 7-9 hours of sleep nightly for better metabolic regulation.
- Maintaining a regular sleep-wake cycle is beneficial for metabolic health.
- Consult healthcare providers before making significant changes to routines.
- Exercise and sleep are integral to a comprehensive health plan including diet and medication.
