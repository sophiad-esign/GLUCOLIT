## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Does impaired fasting glucose confer comparable cardiovascular and mortality risks to diabetes mellitus in patients undergoing peritoneal dialysis?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 216 patients receiving peritoneal dialysis in Taiwan, stratified by baseline glycemic status into normal fasting glucose, impaired fasting glucose, and diabetes mellitus.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Peritoneal dialysis and glucose metabolism disruption due to exposure to glucose-based dialysate.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of outcomes between patients with normal fasting glucose, impaired fasting glucose, and diabetes mellitus.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** All-cause mortality and 3-point major adverse cardiovascular events (3 P-MACE).
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Impaired fasting glucose independently predicted 3 P-MACE and all-cause mortality, with risks comparable to those observed in diabetes mellitus. The TyG index independently predicted both outcomes.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study was conducted in a specific region and population, and the results may not be generalizable to all prediabetes patients. The study shows associations but does not prove causation.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For prediabetes patients undergoing peritoneal dialysis, managing diet and lifestyle is crucial to reducing health risks, and discussions with healthcare providers about these changes are essential.

## English Plain-Language Version

Peritoneal dialysis patients face unique challenges with glucose metabolism due to the use of glucose-based dialysate.

A study of 216 Taiwanese patients found that those with impaired fasting glucose had outcomes as poor as those with diabetes. This suggests the importance of diet and lifestyle adjustments for prediabetes patients on dialysis.

Key measures like the TyG index, which combines triglycerides and glucose, were strong predictors of poor outcomes. This highlights the need for dietary control, especially of fats and sugars.

While the study shows associations, it does not prove causation. The findings are specific to the studied population and may not apply to all prediabetes patients.

However, they do suggest the need for prediabetes patients on dialysis to discuss dietary and lifestyle changes with healthcare providers.

For prediabetes patients on dialysis, consider low-glycemic foods and balanced meals. Regular physical activity can help manage insulin resistance, but always consult with a healthcare provider.

In summary, managing diet and lifestyle is key for prediabetes patients on dialysis to reduce health risks. Work with healthcare providers to find a plan that fits your needs.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Cardiovascular disease is a leading cause of mortality in patients undergoing peritoneal dialysis, and managing prediabetes can significantly impact these risks.

Practical Takeaways

- Prediabetes patients on peritoneal dialysis face similar risks to those with diabetes.
- Managing diet and lifestyle is crucial for prediabetes patients undergoing dialysis.
- Low-glycemic foods and balanced meals are beneficial for blood sugar control.
- Regular physical activity can help manage insulin resistance, but consult with healthcare providers.
