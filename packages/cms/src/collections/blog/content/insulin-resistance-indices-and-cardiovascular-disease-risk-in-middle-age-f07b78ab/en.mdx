## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** How do insulin resistance indices relate to cardiovascular disease risk in middle-aged and older adults with prediabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 3121 participants from the NHANES survey aged 45 and above with prediabetes.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Insulin resistance indices including CTI, TyG, and WWI.
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of cardiovascular disease risk across different levels of insulin resistance indices.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Associations between insulin resistance indices and prevalent cardiovascular disease.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** Higher levels of TyG, TyG-WWI, and CTI were associated with approximately twofold higher CVD risk.
- **局限性 / Limits:** While the study shows associations, it does not establish causation. The study's sample may not be representative of all prediabetic populations.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** For individuals with prediabetes, managing insulin resistance through diet and lifestyle could potentially reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease, but it's important to discuss any changes with a healthcare provider.

## English Plain-Language Version

Imagine you're at a family dinner, and you're told you have prediabetes. The first thought might be fear, but there's also hope.

With the right diet and lifestyle changes, you can manage insulin resistance and reduce your risk of heart disease. A recent study shows that certain insulin resistance indices are linked to higher cardiovascular risks.

Let's look at what this means for your meals and daily routine. The study included over 3000 participants with prediabetes and used machine learning to identify key variables associated with cardiovascular disease.

It found that higher levels of certain insulin resistance indices were associated with about twice the risk of cardiovascular disease. This is a significant finding, but it's important to remember that correlation does not equal causation.

The study's results suggest that diet and lifestyle play a crucial role in managing insulin resistance. For example, swapping out high-sugar foods for complex carbohydrates and adding more fiber to your diet can help.

Regular exercise, even just a brisk walk, can also make a difference. When it comes to sleep, maintaining a consistent schedule and ensuring you get enough quality sleep is key.

These changes are not just about numbers on a scale or a blood test; they're about how you feel and your quality of life. Remember, while this information is based on a study, it's not a prescription.

Always discuss any changes with your healthcare provider. In summary, managing insulin resistance is about more than just diet; it's a holistic approach to your health.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

Insulin resistance is a common issue among individuals with prediabetes, and managing it can prevent the progression to type 2 diabetes and reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease.

This study provides insights into how diet and lifestyle can be leveraged to manage insulin resistance effectively.

Practical Takeaways

- Diet and lifestyle changes can significantly impact insulin resistance and cardiovascular disease risk.
- Simple swaps in your diet, like choosing complex carbs over simple sugars, can make a difference.
- Regular exercise, even moderate, can help manage insulin resistance.
- Maintaining a consistent sleep schedule and quality is crucial for overall health.
- Always consult with a healthcare provider before making significant lifestyle changes.
