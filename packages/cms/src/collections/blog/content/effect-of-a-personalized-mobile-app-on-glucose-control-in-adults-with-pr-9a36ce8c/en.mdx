## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** Can a personalized mobile app improve glucose control in adults with prediabetes and type 2 diabetes?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 21 adults with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Use of the PROTEIN mobile app for personalized meals and exercise recommendations based on continuous glucose monitoring system data
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of time in range (TIR) before and after the 12-week intervention
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Increase in TIR, glycated hemoglobin, fasting blood glucose, and body weight
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** A modest increase in TIR was observed with the use of the PROTEIN app, but no significant changes in other health markers. The dropout rate was high, and the average duration of app use was 42 days.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study had a small sample size and an exploratory design, which limits the generalizability of the findings. The short duration of app use also affects the reliability of the results.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While the PROTEIN app showed some potential in improving time in range, the results should be interpreted with caution due to the study's limitations. It suggests that personalized mobile apps could be a part of future diabetes management strategies, but more research is needed to confirm their effectiveness.

这项研究涉及21名糖尿病前期或2型糖尿病患者，他们使用了连续葡萄糖监测系统以及PROTEIN应用程序。 该应用程序提供个性化的饮食和运动建议，以帮助改善他们的目标范围内时间（TIR），即血糖保持在目标范围内的百分比。

## English Plain-Language Version

Managing blood sugar is like keeping a car in the right speed range. A study tested a mobile app that offers personalized advice on meals and activities based on blood sugar levels.

The app showed promise in helping keep blood sugar in a healthier range, but the results were not conclusive due to a small number of participants and short app usage. It's like trying a new fuel-saving technique for a short trip;

some benefits may be seen, but long-term effectiveness is uncertain without further practice.

In this study, 21 adults with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes used the PROTEIN app along with a continuous glucose monitoring system.

The app provided personalized meal and exercise recommendations to improve their time in range (TIR), the percentage of time blood sugar stays within a target range.

There was a modest increase in TIR, but other health markers like hemoglobin A1c, fasting blood glucose, and body weight did not change significantly.

The study's results are preliminary, and more research is needed to confirm the app's effectiveness.

For now, if you have prediabetes, it's crucial to work with your healthcare provider to develop a personalized diet and exercise plan.

The PROTEIN app's approach could be part of the future of diabetes management, but currently, it's one of many tools that might help.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

A personalized approach to diet and exercise is essential for managing prediabetes and type 2 diabetes.

Understanding the potential of new technologies, such as the PROTEIN app, can assist patients and healthcare providers in making informed decisions about the integration of digital tools into diabetes management.

Practical Takeaways

- Personalized mobile apps offer diet and exercise advice based on glucose levels, but their effectiveness is still under investigation.
- The PROTEIN app showed a modest increase in time in range, yet other health markers remained largely unchanged.
- Further research with larger samples and longer-term use is necessary to establish the benefits of such apps.
- In the interim, individuals with prediabetes should collaborate with healthcare providers to craft personalized diet and exercise plans.
- The integration of technology with personalized health advice may shape the future of diabetes management.
