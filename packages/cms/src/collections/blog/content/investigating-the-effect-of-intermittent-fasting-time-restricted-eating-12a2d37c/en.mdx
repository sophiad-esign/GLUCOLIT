## 审核用证据卡片

- **研究问题 / Research question:** What are the effects of time-restricted eating (TRE) with and without calorie restriction on depression severity scores and metabolic factors in obese patients with major depression disorder?
- **研究对象 / Population:** 90 obese patients with major depression over 24 weeks.
- **干预或暴露 / Intervention or exposure:** Three groups: TRE with 8-hour food intake restriction, TRE with 8-hour food intake restriction and calorie restriction (75% of required calories), and calorie restriction (75% of required calories).
- **对照 / Comparison:** Comparison of TRE with and without calorie restriction to traditional calorie restriction.
- **观察指标 / Outcomes:** Changes in depression severity scores, body weight, BMI, waist circumference, fat mass, fat-free mass, circadian rhythm score, quality of life, physical activity, fasting glucose levels, serum insulin, hemoglobin A1C, insulin resistance and sensitivity, lipid profile, and blood pressure.
- **主要发现 / Main findings:** The study aims to assess the primary objective of changes in depression severity scores alongside secondary outcomes including various metabolic factors.
- **局限性 / Limits:** The study is ongoing, and results are not yet available. The impact of TRE on mental health is still not fully understood.
- **读者怎么理解 / Reader meaning:** While TRE shows promise in improving body weight and metabolic parameters, its effects on mental health, especially in the context of major depression disorder, are still under investigation. Readers should be cautious about overclaiming the benefits of TRE until more data is available from this and other studies.

## English Plain-Language Version

A new dietary approach called time-restricted eating (TRE) is being studied for its effects on weight and mental health. TRE sets an 8-hour window for all food intake, aligning with our natural circadian rhythms.

This method could be particularly beneficial for those with metabolic issues and mental health challenges.

Traditional calorie restriction has shown inconsistent effects on mental health, while TRE shows promise in improving body weight and metabolic parameters without strict calorie counting.

However, the impact of TRE on mental health is still not fully understood and requires further research.

This study aims to compare TRE with and without calorie restriction to traditional calorie restriction in obese patients with major depressive disorder.

The results could provide valuable insights into how TRE might be used as part of an integrated approach to managing both obesity and depression. For those considering TRE, it's crucial to start slowly and listen to your body.

Any significant changes to your diet or lifestyle should be discussed with a healthcare provider.

Why This Matters for Prediabetes

For individuals with prediabetes and major depression disorder, managing both conditions can be challenging.

This study provides insights into how lifestyle interventions, specifically time-restricted eating, might offer a dual approach to improving mental health and metabolic health.

Practical Takeaways

- Time-restricted eating (TRE) is a structured fasting approach that may benefit mental health and metabolic factors.
- TRE involves an 8-hour eating window, aligning with our natural circadian rhythms.
- Traditional calorie restriction has shown inconsistent effects on mental health, while TRE shows promise.
- The impact of TRE on mental health is not fully understood and requires further research.
- Consider TRE as a potential part of an integrated approach to managing obesity and depression.
